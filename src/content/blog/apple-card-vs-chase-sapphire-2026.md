# Apple Card vs Chase Sapphire Preferred 2026: Rewards, Fees, and Which Card Wins

**Apple Card vs Chase Sapphire Preferred** is a matchup between two very different philosophies of what a credit card should be. Apple Card is a no-annual-fee card designed to live inside the Apple ecosystem, paying you Daily Cash on every purchase. Chase Sapphire Preferred is a travel rewards card with an annual fee, a sign-up bonus, and a points system built for people who want to turn everyday spending into flights and hotel stays.

They overlap on exactly one thing: both earn rewards on purchases. After that, they diverge sharply. This guide compares rewards structure, fees, APR, sign-up bonuses, travel perks, and ecosystem benefits side by side so you can figure out which card fits the way you actually spend money. None of this is financial advice — just a neutral feature comparison. If you are also evaluating cards without annual fees for everyday categories, our [best credit card for groceries with no annual fee](/blog/best-credit-card-for-groceries-no-annual-fee-2026/) breakdown is worth a look.

## Rewards Structure: Cash Back vs Flexible Points

This is the core trade-off. Apple Card pays you in cash. Chase Sapphire Preferred pays you in points that can be worth more than their face value if you use them strategically.

**Apple Card** uses a tiered Daily Cash system. You earn 3% back when you buy directly from Apple or from select partner merchants like Uber, T-Mobile, Walgreens, Nike, and Exxon. Everything you pay for through Apple Pay earns 2%. Purchases made with the physical titanium card earn 1%. The cash hits your Apple Cash balance every day — no waiting for a statement cycle, no minimum redemption threshold, no expiration.

**Chase Sapphire Preferred** earns 5x points on travel purchased through Chase Travel, 3x on dining (restaurants, takeout, delivery), 3x on select streaming services, 3x on online grocery purchases (excluding in-store), and 1x on everything else. Points are worth at least 1 cent each when redeemed for cash, but they are worth 25% more when redeemed through Chase Travel. Transfer them to airline or hotel partners — Hyatt, United, Southwest, British Airways, and others — and their value can stretch further depending on how you book.

The practical difference: if you spend $2,000 a month and most of it goes through Apple Pay, Apple Card gives you roughly $40 in cash back every month with zero effort. Chase Sapphire Preferred on the same spend could earn you more value, but only if you funnel spending through bonus categories and redeem points through travel partners rather than taking the cash option. Points flexibility is powerful, but it requires more intention.

## Annual Fee: $0 vs $95

Apple Card charges no annual fee. Chase Sapphire Preferred charges $95 per year.

That $95 is easy to justify if you use the card's travel perks, but it does mean Chase Sapphire Preferred needs to earn you at least $95 more in value than a free card would. The $50 annual Chase Travel hotel credit knocks the effective fee down to $45, and a single good points transfer redemption can cover the rest. But if you are not going to book travel through Chase or transfer points to partners, that $95 is a harder sell.

Apple Card's $0 fee means it never costs you money to keep it open. There is no mental math about whether the card "pays for itself." You just use it, earn Daily Cash, and move on.

## APR: Neither Card Is Cheap to Carry a Balance

Both cards charge variable APR that adjusts with the prime rate, and neither is a low-interest card. If you carry a balance month to month, the interest will eat your rewards regardless of which card you hold.

Apple Card does offer one structural advantage here: the Wallet app shows you how different payment amounts affect the interest you will pay, and it nudges you to pay more than the minimum. It will not lower your rate, but the transparency helps some people make better decisions about paying down balances.

Chase Sapphire Preferred does not have the same built-in payment calculator, but Chase's app lets you set up autopay at the full statement balance, which avoids interest entirely.

The bottom line on APR: both cards assume you pay in full each month. Their rewards math only works if you do.

## Sign-Up Bonus: Chase's Big Lead

**Apple Card has no sign-up bonus.** There is no spend threshold, no limited-time offer. You get the card, you start earning Daily Cash from purchase one.

**Chase Sapphire Preferred typically offers a large sign-up bonus** — a substantial number of Ultimate Rewards points after you spend a set amount within the first three months. The exact figures change periodically, but the bonus alone can be worth several hundred dollars in travel, and it is one of the most compelling reasons to open the card.

If sign-up bonuses matter to you, Chase Sapphire Preferred is in a different league. Apple Card does not compete here at all.

## Travel Perks: Chase Sapphire Preferred Runs the Table

If you travel more than once or twice a year, Chase Sapphire Preferred packs in a set of benefits that Apple Card simply does not offer.

**Chase Sapphire Preferred travel benefits include:**

- **Trip cancellation and interruption insurance** — reimbursement if a covered trip is canceled or cut short
- **Baggage delay insurance** — coverage for essentials if your luggage is delayed
- **Primary car rental collision damage waiver** — covers damage to a rental car so you can decline the rental company's insurance
- **No foreign transaction fees** — spend abroad without a surcharge
- **$50 annual Chase Travel hotel credit** — applied automatically when booking through Chase Travel
- **25% points bonus** when redeeming through Chase Travel

**Apple Card travel benefits:**

- **No foreign transaction fees** — and that is about it

Apple Card does not include trip insurance, rental car coverage, baggage protection, or any travel credits. ## Apple Ecosystem vs Chase Ecosystem

Both cards gain additional value when you use them within their respective platforms.

**Apple Card ties into the Apple ecosystem tightly.** Daily Cash lands in your Apple Cash balance, which you can spend with Apple Pay, send to friends, or transfer to your bank. You can also funnel it into an Apple Savings account, which earns a competitive APY — making Apple Card a quiet little savings machine for people already in the Apple world. For a deeper look at how Apple Savings compares to standalone high-yield accounts, see our [Apple Savings vs HYSA 2026](/blog/apple-savings-vs-hysa-2026/) comparison.

The card itself lives natively in Apple Wallet with real-time spending breakdowns, color-coded purchase categories, and payment scheduling. There is no separate app to download. If you own an iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Apple Watch, the card feels like a natural extension of your devices.

**Chase Sapphire Preferred connects to the Chase Ultimate Rewards ecosystem.** That means access to over a dozen airline and hotel transfer partners, the ability to combine points with a Chase Freedom card for higher earning, and the Pay Yourself Back feature that lets you use points as statement credits toward select categories. If you later upgrade to Chase Sapphire Reserve, your entire points balance comes with you and becomes even more valuable.

The Chase ecosystem is more complex, but the ceiling is higher. A well-managed Chase card portfolio (Sapphire Preferred plus a Freedom card, for instance) can generate serious travel value. Apple Card's ecosystem is simpler and more self-contained, but the ceiling is lower.

## Who Should Pick Apple Card?

Apple Card is the better fit if you:

- Use an iPhone and Apple Pay for most of your purchases
- Want a no-annual-fee card that earns decent cash back with zero complexity
- Prefer Daily Cash deposited automatically over tracking points
- Shop regularly at Apple, Uber, T-Mobile, Nike, or other 3% partners
- Want to route your rewards into an Apple Savings account for passive yield
- Do not travel enough to justify paying $95 a year for travel perks

Apple Card rewards simplicity. You tap your phone, you get cash back, the money lands in your account the same day. No annual fee math, no partner transfer charts, no sign-up bonus deadlines.

---

## Who Should Pick Chase Sapphire Preferred?

Chase Sapphire Preferred is the better fit if you:

- Travel at least a few times a year and want trip insurance and rental car coverage
- Spend heavily on dining, takeout, delivery, and streaming
- Value flexible points that can transfer to airline and hotel partners
- Want a large sign-up bonus to jumpstart your rewards balance
- Plan to pair it with a Chase Freedom card for a combined points strategy
- Are willing to pay $95 a year because the travel benefits return more than that

Chase Sapphire Preferred rewards intentionality. You earn more if you optimize your spending categories and redeem through the right channels. The payoff is higher, but it asks more of you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I have both Apple Card and Chase Sapphire Preferred?

Yes. There is no rule against holding both. Some people use Apple Card for everyday Apple Pay purchases and Chase Sapphire Preferred for dining and travel. Since Apple Card has no annual fee, keeping it open alongside a Chase card costs nothing extra.

### Which card is better for groceries?

Neither is a dedicated grocery card. Apple Card earns 2% on groceries bought through Apple Pay. Chase Sapphire Preferred earns 3x on online grocery orders but only 1x on in-store grocery purchases. If groceries are a major category for you, a specialized card might earn more — see our [best credit card for groceries with no annual fee](/blog/best-credit-card-for-groceries-no-annual-fee-2026/) roundup.

### Does Apple Card have travel insurance?

No. Apple Card does not include trip cancellation, baggage delay, or rental car insurance. Chase Sapphire Preferred includes all three. If travel protection matters, Chase is the stronger option.

### Is the Chase Sapphire Preferred annual fee worth it?

It depends on your spending and travel habits. The $50 Chase Travel hotel credit reduces the effective fee to $45. If you redeem points through Chase Travel or transfer partners, the 25% points bonus and partner redemptions can easily return more than $95 in value over a year. If you mostly want cash back and rarely travel, the fee may not pay for itself.

### Can I transfer Apple Card rewards to airlines?

No. Apple Card rewards are paid as Daily Cash — real dollars deposited to your Apple Cash balance. There is no points currency and no transfer partners. Chase Sapphire Preferred points can transfer to over a dozen airline and hotel programs.

## The Bottom Line

Apple Card and Chase Sapphire Preferred are built for different people with different priorities.

- **Choose Apple Card** if you want effortless, fee-free cash back that flows into the Apple ecosystem — especially if you already live on Apple Pay and do not need travel perks.
- **Choose Chase Sapphire Preferred** if you want flexible points that can transfer to travel partners, a meaningful sign-up bonus, and built-in trip insurance — and you are comfortable paying $95 a year for that value.

Neither card is the wrong choice. Apple Card wins on simplicity and zero cost. Chase Sapphire Preferred wins on travel value and points flexibility. Pick the one that matches how you spend, not how you wish you spent.
