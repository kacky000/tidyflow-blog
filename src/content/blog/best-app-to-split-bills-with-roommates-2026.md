# Best App to Split Bills With Roommates in 2026

Finding the **best app to split bills with roommates** comes down to one thing: keeping a clear, fair record of who owes what without it turning into a monthly argument. Rent, utilities, groceries, the shared streaming bundle — when several people share costs unevenly, memory and group chats fail fast. The right app tracks every shared expense automatically and tells each person their exact balance.

This guide compares the five apps roommates actually use in 2026, ranked by how well they handle recurring shared bills, uneven splits, and settling up. Nothing here is sponsored — it is a practical, neutral comparison. ## 1. Splitwise — Best for Tracking Who Owes What

Splitwise is the category leader for a reason: it is built specifically to **track shared expenses and running balances** between people. You add each bill, choose how to split it (equally, by exact amounts, by percentage, or by shares), and Splitwise keeps a live tally of who owes whom. At the end of the month, it tells everyone the single net amount to settle, instead of a tangle of small transfers.

The free tier covers everything most roommate households need: unlimited expense logging, uneven splits, and a group ledger. Splitwise does not move money itself — it links out to payment apps to actually settle — which is exactly why it pairs so well with Venmo or Cash App. Use Splitwise as the **ledger**, and a payment app as the **wallet**. For a deeper look at when a dedicated tracker beats just paying ad hoc, see our [Splitwise vs Venmo](/blog/splitwise-vs-venmo/) breakdown.

## 2. Venmo — Best for Paying Instantly (and Splitting in Groups)

Venmo's strength is **frictionless payments plus a light social layer**. You can request money from a roommate in seconds, split a single charge among several people, and use Venmo Groups to track a shared tab for a trip or a household over time. For roommates who mostly need to *pay each other fast*, Venmo is often enough on its own.

Watch the fees: standard transfers from your Venmo balance or linked bank account are free, but **instant transfers to your bank carry a fee**, and paying with a credit card adds a surcharge. Stick to bank/debit and standard withdrawals to keep it free. Venmo is the best all-rounder when you want tracking and payment in one app, even if its tracking is lighter than Splitwise's. To see how it stacks up against bank-based transfers, our [Venmo vs Zelle](/blog/venmo-vs-zelle/) guide covers the fee details.

## 3. Cash App — Best for Simple Peer Payments

Cash App keeps things minimal: send and request money by $Cashtag, fast and free on standard transfers. It does not track running balances or shared tabs, so it is not a bill-management tool — it is a **wallet**. For a small household that pays each bill immediately and never carries an IOU, that simplicity is a feature, not a flaw.

Cash App adds extras like a debit card and stock/Bitcoin buying, which some roommates already use. If your group only needs to move money and you are settling in real time, Cash App is a clean choice. If you tend to carry balances across the month, pair it with a tracker. For how it compares to a banking-style alternative, see [Chime vs Cash App](/blog/chime-vs-cash-app/).

## 4. Zelle — Best for No-Balance Bank Transfers

Zelle moves money **directly between U.S. bank accounts**, usually within minutes, with no app balance to manage and no transfer fees from Zelle itself. For roommates who all bank with major institutions, Zelle is the most "invisible" option — the money simply lands in the recipient's checking account.

The trade-off is that Zelle does **zero tracking**. There is no ledger, no split feature, no history beyond your bank statement. It is purely a transfer rail. Use Zelle to *settle* a balance that you tracked elsewhere (a spreadsheet or Splitwise), not to manage the splits themselves. One caution: Zelle payments are instant and effectively irreversible, so double-check the recipient.

## 5. A Shared Spreadsheet — Best for Total Control at $0

Not every household wants another app. A **shared spreadsheet** (Google Sheets) gives you complete control: custom categories, exact percentages, notes on who paid the deposit, and a permanent record — all for free, with no account anyone has to install. It is the most flexible option for complex or long-running arrangements.

The cost is effort: someone has to maintain it, and there are no automatic reminders. For households that want structure without a monthly subscription, a ready-made template removes the setup work. ## How to Choose the Right Setup

The best roommate households usually combine **two tools**, not one:

- **A ledger** to track who owes what over the month — Splitwise or a spreadsheet.
- **A wallet** to actually move money when you settle — Venmo, Cash App, or Zelle.

Match the combo to your habits:

- **You carry balances all month** → Splitwise (ledger) + Venmo/Zelle (settle).
- **You pay every bill on the spot** → Venmo or Cash App alone is enough.
- **Your splits are uneven or complex** → Spreadsheet + the [bill split calculator](/tools/bill-split-calculator/).
- **Everyone uses the same big bank** → Zelle to settle, tracked anywhere.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best free app to split bills with roommates?

Splitwise is the best free option for *tracking* who owes what, with unlimited expenses and uneven splits on its free tier. For *paying*, Venmo, Cash App, and Zelle are all free on standard bank transfers. Most roommates use Splitwise to track and one of the others to settle.

### Can I split bills unevenly between roommates?

Yes. Splitwise supports splitting by exact amounts, percentages, or shares, which is ideal when rooms are different sizes or income differs. A spreadsheet or our [bill split calculator](/tools/bill-split-calculator/) also handles uneven splits. See our [how to split rent unevenly](/blog/how-to-split-rent-unevenly/) guide for fair methods.

### Is Splitwise or Venmo better for roommates?

Splitwise is better for *tracking* ongoing shared expenses and net balances; Venmo is better for *paying* instantly. Many households use both. Our [Splitwise vs Venmo](/blog/splitwise-vs-venmo/) comparison breaks down exactly which to use when.

### Do these apps charge fees?

Tracking in Splitwise is free. Venmo, Cash App, and Zelle are free on standard bank/debit transfers, but Venmo and Cash App charge for instant transfers, and credit-card payments add a surcharge. Zelle itself charges no fees.
