Personal Capital — now rebranded as **Empower Personal Dashboard** — is one of the most powerful free financial tools available. Notion is the ultimate flexible workspace. Both can serve as your budgeting hub, but they do fundamentally different jobs.

This comparison gives you a clear picture of what each tool does well, where each falls short, and which one matches your actual budgeting style.

## Quick Context: Personal Capital Became Empower

In 2023, Personal Capital rebranded to **Empower Personal Dashboard** after acquisition. The free tier (budgeting, net worth tracking, investment analysis) remains available and is what most people mean when they say "Personal Capital." The Empower wealth management services require $100,000+ in assets and human advisors — not relevant for most budgeters.

Throughout this article, "Personal Capital / Empower" refers to the **free personal finance dashboard**.

## The Core Difference

**Personal Capital / Empower** is a read-only financial dashboard. It aggregates your accounts, shows spending, tracks net worth, analyzes investments, and provides insights — automatically, with no manual entry.

**Notion** is a blank-canvas workspace where you build your budget system manually. Every dollar you track requires intentional entry or import.

These tools aren't really competing for the same user. But many people choose one or the other as their primary financial hub — so the comparison matters.

## Feature-by-Feature Comparison

### Net Worth Tracking

**Personal Capital / Empower: 10/10**

This is Empower's killer feature. Connect all accounts (checking, savings, investment, retirement, mortgage, credit cards) and get a real-time net worth dashboard updated automatically. Beautifully designed, extremely accurate.

**Notion: 5/10**

You can build a net worth tracker in Notion, but you must update it manually. Works fine for quarterly check-ins; impractical for real-time tracking.

**Winner: Personal Capital / Empower — no contest**

### Investment Analysis

**Personal Capital / Empower: 9/10**

The Investment Checkup and Fee Analyzer tools are genuinely valuable. Empower shows your asset allocation, compares it to an age-appropriate target, and identifies hidden mutual fund fees. The Retirement Planner is one of the best free retirement calculators available.

**Notion: 2/10**

You can log investments in a Notion database, but there's no analysis, no fee detection, and no portfolio modeling. Notion doesn't know what an index fund is.

**Winner: Personal Capital / Empower**

### Monthly Budget Tracking

**Personal Capital / Empower: 6/10**

Empower's budget feature is functional but not its strength. Transactions auto-categorize (with errors), and you can set category limits and see spending vs budget. It works, but users often find it clunky — you can't easily rearrange categories or build custom views.

**Notion: 9/10**

Build exactly the budget structure you need. Create custom categories, build linked databases for income/expenses/savings, build monthly reviews, track progress with progress bars. The flexibility is unlimited.

**Winner: Notion (for active monthly budgeting)**

### Customization

**Personal Capital / Empower: 4/10**

Empower gives you a standardized dashboard. You can hide accounts, adjust categories (with friction), and set budget limits — but the underlying structure is fixed. Power budgeters hit walls quickly.

**Notion: 10/10**

If you can imagine it, you can build it. Custom categories, linked databases, Kanban views for financial goals, formula fields for automatic calculations — Notion is limited only by your imagination and time.

**Winner: Notion**

### Privacy and Data Security

**Personal Capital / Empower: 5/10**

Connecting accounts requires storing bank credentials with a third party. Empower uses Yodlee/Plaid for account aggregation — industry standard, but not zero risk. Read-only access minimizes exposure, but the data is still on Empower's servers.

**Notion: 8/10**

Notion never touches your bank accounts. You type in numbers manually — no credentials, no read access to financial accounts. Your data lives in Notion's database, not a financial aggregator.

**Winner: Notion**

### Cost

**Personal Capital / Empower: 10/10 (free tier)**

The personal finance dashboard is completely free. Empower makes money from wealth management upsells (they will contact you if you have significant assets). For basic budgeting and tracking, the cost is zero.

**Notion: 9/10**

Notion's free personal plan covers everything an individual budgeter needs. If you want collaboration features, the paid plan is $10/month.

**Winner: Tie (both free for personal use)**

### Ease of Use

**Personal Capital / Empower: 8/10**

Connect accounts, and the dashboard builds itself. Minimal setup required. Most users can have a functional financial overview in 30 minutes.

**Notion: 5/10**

Building a Notion budget takes 2-4 hours upfront. Starting from a template cuts this to 30-60 minutes, but there's still a learning curve.

**Winner: Personal Capital / Empower (for getting started)**

## Who Should Use Personal Capital / Empower

Choose Empower when:
- You want automatic net worth tracking with zero effort
- You actively invest and want portfolio analysis
- You want to check if your mutual funds have hidden fees
- You're planning for retirement and want modeling tools
- You're okay with connecting bank accounts to a third-party tool

Empower is especially powerful for **high earners who are building wealth** but don't want to spend hours on detailed budgeting. The Investment Checkup alone can save thousands in unnecessary fees.

## Who Should Use Notion

Choose Notion when:
- You want full control over your budget structure
- Privacy is more important than automation
- You're already using Notion for other systems
- You need custom categories that financial tools don't offer
- You enjoy designing and iterating systems

Notion works best for **intentional budgeters** who think carefully about every spending category and want to build a system that evolves with their life.

## The Ideal Setup: Use Both

This is actually the most common answer among serious personal finance enthusiasts:

1. **Empower** for the macro view: net worth, investment health, retirement projection — all automated
2. **Notion** for the micro view: monthly budget planning, expense tracking, financial goals, and notes

Check Empower monthly to verify the big picture. Use Notion weekly for intentional budget management. The combination gives you automation where it matters (net worth, investments) and flexibility where it matters (monthly spending decisions).

## FAQs

**Is Personal Capital / Empower safe?**
It uses read-only access to your accounts through Plaid — a standard, widely-used bank connection service. It can view transactions but cannot initiate transfers. Most major banks (Chase, BoA, Wells Fargo) explicitly support Plaid connections. The risk is real but minimal for read-only access.

**Did Personal Capital change its name?**
Yes. Personal Capital was acquired by Empower Retirement in 2020 and rebranded to Empower Personal Dashboard in 2023. The product is largely the same; the brand changed.

**Can I use Notion instead of Personal Capital for net worth tracking?**
Yes, but it requires manual updates. Most users update quarterly. If you're okay with that, Notion works fine. If you want real-time automatic updates, Personal Capital / Empower is dramatically better.
