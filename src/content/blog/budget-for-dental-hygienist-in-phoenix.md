Phoenix is one of the fastest-growing cities in the United States, and that growth is creating strong demand for dental hygienists. If you're working or considering working as a DH in the Phoenix metro, here's exactly what your budget looks like — including Arizona's newly simplified tax structure.

## What Dental Hygienists Earn in Phoenix

BLS data shows dental hygienists in the Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler metro earn between **$68,000 and $87,000** annually. The median is approximately **$75,000/year**, with specialty and corporate dental groups sometimes offering higher.

**Estimated monthly take-home (at $75K gross, Arizona 2.5% flat tax):**
- Federal income tax: ~$1,020/month
- Arizona state tax (2.5% flat): ~$156/month
- FICA: ~$480/month
- Health insurance: ~$160/month
- **Estimated net: ~$4,600–$4,900/month**

Arizona switched to a flat 2.5% income tax in 2023, making it one of the most tax-friendly states for middle-income workers.

## Student Loan Repayment in Phoenix

Most dental hygiene programs run $25,000–$55,000 in total debt. In Phoenix, the main options:

**IDR + PSLF Path**
Maricopa Integrated Health System (MIHS), Valleywise Health, and Arizona State University's dental clinic are FQHC or government employers — meaning PSLF-eligible. See the [full PSLF guide](/pslf-loan-forgiveness-guide-for-public-service-workers/).

**Aggressive Payoff Path**
At $4,750 net, a hygienist renting in Mesa ($1,400) could put $700–$800/month toward loans and pay off $50K in 6–7 years while still saving for retirement.

## Where Phoenix Dental Hygienists Lose Money

**Problem #1: Utilities underestimation**
New-to-Phoenix hygienists routinely underestimate summer cooling costs. Budget $200/month in summer months (June–Sep), not $100.

**Problem #2: Car dependency with long commutes**
Phoenix is sprawling. Your commute from Glendale to a Scottsdale specialty office might be 45 minutes each way. This adds gas and wear costs — plus makes 2-office work difficult.

**Problem #3: Lifestyle spending from the restaurant scene**
Tempe and Scottsdale have booming food/entertainment scenes. It's easy to spend $600–$800/month on dining and entertainment when you meant to spend $300.

## Phoenix Job Market for DHs

- **Corporate dental groups** (Aspen, Pacific, Smile Brands): Very active in Phoenix. Consistent schedules, lower pay ceiling
- **Private practices in North Scottsdale/Chandler**: Higher pay, often more patient-focused care
- **Valleywise Health (MIHS)**: County hospital system = PSLF eligible
- **Arizona State University Dental Clinic**: University = PSLF eligible

The Phoenix metro adds 80,000+ new residents annually, making dental demand structural, not cyclical.
