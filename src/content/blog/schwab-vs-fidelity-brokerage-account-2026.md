# Schwab vs Fidelity Brokerage Account 2026: Fees, Features, and Which Broker Fits You

**Schwab vs Fidelity brokerage account** — this is the matchup that defines serious, low-cost investing in the United States. Charles Schwab and Fidelity Investments are the two largest retail brokerages by client assets, each serving tens of millions of accounts with $0 commissions, deep fund lineups, strong research, and nationwide branch networks. They agree on so many headline features that choosing between them often comes down to quieter differences: how your idle cash is handled, which trading platform you prefer, and whether you care about zero-expense-ratio funds.

This is a neutral, practical comparison — not investment advice. We will walk through commissions, account types, tools, fractional shares, cash management, and service so you can decide which brokerage fits the way you actually invest. ## Commission and Fee Structure

Both Schwab and Fidelity charge **$0 commissions on U.S. stocks, ETFs, and most mutual funds**. Options cost **$0.65 per contract** at each broker — identical to the penny. Neither charges annual maintenance fees or inactivity penalties on standard brokerage accounts, and neither requires a minimum balance to open one.

The meaningful cost gap is not in trading — it is in **fund expense ratios** and **idle cash yield**. Fidelity's zero-expense-ratio index funds (FZROX, FNILX) literally charge nothing, while Schwab's cheapest index funds (SWPPX at 0.02%, SWTSX at 0.03%) are ultra-low but not free. On a $100,000 balance the difference is roughly $20–$30 per year — small, but it compounds. Schwab counters with its free robo-advisor, Intelligent Portfolios, which has no advisory fee at all above a $5,000 minimum. ## Research and Trading Tools

This is the category where the brokers diverge most clearly.

**Schwab** now owns **Thinkorswim**, the professional-grade trading platform acquired through the TD Ameritrade merger. Thinkorswim offers advanced charting, options strategy builders, paper trading, and deep customization — it is widely regarded as one of the best active-trading platforms available to retail investors. If you trade options frequently or rely on technical analysis, Thinkorswim is a genuine competitive advantage.

**Fidelity** offers **Active Trader Pro**, a solid desktop platform with real-time streaming, advanced order types, and good screening tools. It is capable, but it does not match Thinkorswim's depth for power users. Where Fidelity pulls ahead is the **everyday investor experience** — its mobile app is consistently top-rated for clarity, ease of navigation, and integrated research from multiple independent providers.

The split is straightforward: **Schwab for active and options traders** who want Thinkorswim; **Fidelity for long-term investors** who want clean research and a polished app without complexity.

## Fractional Shares and Investing Features

Both brokers let you buy slices of expensive stocks, but the scope differs.

- **Schwab Stock Slices** lets you buy fractional shares of **S&P 500 stocks** starting at $5. The selection is limited to the 500 companies in the index — you cannot buy fractional shares of smaller or international stocks.
- **Fidelity Stocks by the Slice** covers a **much broader universe** — thousands of U.S. stocks and ETFs are eligible for fractional purchases starting at $1.

For an investor who wants to dollar-cost average into a diversified mix of individual names, Fidelity's wider fractional coverage is a practical advantage. If you only buy large-cap names or prefer index funds and ETFs (which are already inherently diversified), Schwab's S&P 500 limitation is unlikely to matter. Both brokers also support automatic recurring investments, making it easy to set contributions on autopilot.

## Cash Management and Banking

Cash handling is one of the quietest but most impactful differences between these two brokers.

**Schwab** has a full bank — **Charles Schwab Bank** — and offers the Schwab Bank High Yield Investor Checking account, which comes with unlimited ATM fee rebates worldwide and no foreign transaction fees. It is one of the best checking accounts for travelers. However, Schwab's default **cash sweep in brokerage accounts** sends idle funds to a bank sweep that typically yields less than money market rates.

**Fidelity** offers the **Fidelity Cash Management Account**, which also provides ATM fee reimbursements, no monthly fees, and bill pay. The key difference: Fidelity's brokerage idle cash defaults to a **money market sweep** that generally earns a higher yield automatically — no manual action needed.

If you keep any meaningful cash buffer in your brokerage account, Fidelity's default behavior puts more money in your pocket. Schwab can match the yield, but you have to actively purchase a money market fund. For investors who also want a strong checking experience, Schwab's worldwide ATM rebates and integrated banking make it a compelling package.

## Customer Service and Branch Access

Both firms maintain large customer-service operations with **24/7 phone support**, online chat, and extensive help centers. Independent surveys consistently rate both highly — this is not a category where either broker lags.

**Schwab** operates roughly **300+ branches** across the U.S., partly expanded through the TD Ameritrade merger. **Fidelity** has **200+ Investor Centers**. If in-person help matters to you — opening a complex trust account, discussing estate planning, or getting face-to-face guidance — check which firm has a location near you, since coverage varies by metro area.

Both offer **SIPC protection** (up to $500,000, including $250,000 in cash) and carry supplemental insurance beyond SIPC limits. On safety and regulatory standing, they are peers. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Schwab or Fidelity cheaper overall?

Headline trading costs are identical — $0 for stocks and ETFs, $0.65 per options contract. Fidelity edges ahead on fund costs (zero-expense-ratio index funds) and idle-cash yield (higher-yielding default sweep). Schwab counters with a free robo-advisor and integrated banking. For most buy-and-hold investors, Fidelity has a slight cost advantage; for banking-heavy users, Schwab can be more convenient.

### Which is better for retirement accounts?

Both offer the same IRA types with no minimums. Fidelity's zero-fee funds lower the cost of a core retirement portfolio to literally nothing. Schwab's Intelligent Portfolios provides free automated management for those who want a hands-off retirement strategy. Either is a strong choice — pick based on whether you prefer DIY fund selection (Fidelity) or automated management (Schwab).

### Can I transfer from Schwab to Fidelity (or vice versa)?

Yes. Both support ACATS transfers, which move most assets in-kind (without selling) in about five to seven business days. The receiving broker sometimes offers transfer-fee reimbursement — check current promotions before you start. Note that Fidelity's proprietary zero-fee funds cannot transfer in-kind to Schwab; they would need to be sold first.

### Should I keep accounts at both?

Some investors do. A common setup is Fidelity for core index investing and cash management, paired with Schwab for Thinkorswim-based active trading or the banking ecosystem. There is no rule against it, though a single brokerage is simpler for most people.

## Verdict: Which Broker Is Better for You?

There is no universal winner — the right choice depends on how you invest.

**Choose Schwab** if you are an active or options trader who wants Thinkorswim, if you value integrated banking with worldwide ATM rebates, or if you prefer a free robo-advisor with no advisory fee. Schwab is also a natural fit if you already bank with them and want everything under one roof.

**Choose Fidelity** if you are a long-term, hands-off investor who wants zero-expense-ratio index funds, a higher-yielding cash default, and a polished mobile app. Fidelity is the lower-friction choice for someone who wants to set up automatic contributions and rarely log in.

The honest reality is that the gap between Schwab and Fidelity is far smaller than the gap between investing consistently and not investing at all. Both are top-tier brokerages with decades of track records, strong protections, and low costs. Pick the one that matches your habits, automate your contributions, and focus your energy on the decisions that actually move the needle — like how much you save each month and how long you stay invested.
