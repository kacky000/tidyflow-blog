# Simplifi by Quicken vs Notion for Budgeting: Which Is Better in 2026?

**Simplifi by Quicken** is a sleek, subscription-based budgeting app designed for people who want automatic bank feeds, spending plans, and clean visualizations without much setup. **Notion** is a blank-canvas workspace that you configure into whatever budgeting system you want — with full control and no monthly fee.

They solve the same problem in fundamentally different ways. This comparison will help you pick the right tool for how you actually budget.

## What Is Simplifi?

Simplifi by Quicken launched in 2020 as Quicken's attempt at a modern, simple budgeting app — think Mint's successor with cleaner design. It connects to your bank accounts, automatically categorizes transactions, and builds a "Spending Plan" based on your income and fixed bills.

**Simplifi's core features:**
- Automatic bank account sync (10,000+ institutions)
- Customizable spending plan (not a traditional category budget)
- Watchlists for specific spending categories
- Savings goals tracking
- Bill tracking and upcoming payment reminders
- Investment account overview (net worth)
- iOS and Android apps

**Pricing:** $3.99/month (billed annually at $47.88) or $5.99/month billed monthly. There's a 30-day free trial.

---

## What Is Notion for Budgeting?

Notion is a general-purpose workspace tool that many people have configured into powerful budgeting systems using databases, formulas, and linked views. Unlike Simplifi, Notion doesn't know anything about banking — you bring your own data.

**Notion's budgeting capabilities:**
- Custom database for income and expense tracking
- Formulas for automatic calculations (totals, remaining budget, percentages)
- Multiple views of the same data (table, calendar, gallery, kanban)
- Linked databases for multi-month tracking
- Templates from Notion's template gallery or third-party creators
- Integration with other tools via Notion API

**Pricing:** Free plan (limited blocks), Plus at $10/month, Business at $15/month. Most budgeters use the free plan successfully.

## Head-to-Head Comparison

### Bank Sync & Transaction Import

**Simplifi wins here — it's not close.**

Simplifi syncs with 10,000+ financial institutions in real time. Transactions appear automatically, pre-categorized (though you'll refine categories over time). You can review transactions, split them, add notes, and mark them as reviewed.

Notion has no bank connectivity whatsoever. You manually type in or paste transactions. Some users import via CSV and upload to a Notion database. A few third-party tools (like Zapier + your bank's CSV export) can semi-automate this, but it's never seamless.

If you want automation, Simplifi is the clear choice.

---

### Budgeting Approach

**Simplifi's Spending Plan** isn't a traditional category budget — it's more of a cash flow tracker. You tell it your income sources and fixed bills, and it shows how much "flexible" money remains. It's elegant but opinionated.

**Notion** lets you build any budgeting method you want: zero-based budgeting, 50/30/20, envelope system, cash stuffing tracker, or a completely custom approach. Our [50/30/20 budget rule guide](/blog/50-30-20-budget-rule-notion/) shows one example of a Notion budget build.

If your budgeting needs are standard, Simplifi's approach works great. If you have a specific method or unusual financial setup, Notion's flexibility wins.

---

### Customization

Simplifi lets you customize categories, rename accounts, and set watchlists. But the core product structure is fixed — you can't rearrange the dashboard, add new view types, or build multi-year tracking.

Notion is infinitely customizable. Build dashboards, add custom formulas, create linked databases for business vs. personal tracking, and design the exact views you want. The tradeoff: you have to build it (or use a template).

**Winner: Notion** — if customization matters to you.

---

### Mobile Experience

Both have solid mobile apps, but Simplifi's is genuinely excellent. The spending plan updates in real time as transactions come in, and the mobile review workflow is intuitive.

Notion's mobile app is good but optimized for editing, not quick financial review. If you want to quickly check your spending while out, Simplifi is faster.

**Winner: Simplifi** for quick mobile check-ins.

---

### Data Ownership & Privacy

With Simplifi, your financial data lives on Quicken's servers. You're trusting them with your bank credentials (via Plaid or direct bank connections). Simplifi's export options are limited.

With Notion, your budget lives in your Notion account. You can export everything as CSV or Markdown anytime. You never share bank credentials with any third party.

**Winner: Notion** for people concerned about financial data privacy.

---

### Investment & Net Worth Tracking

Simplifi includes investment account connections and a net worth overview — you can see savings, investments, debt, and checking all in one place.

Notion doesn't natively connect to investment accounts. You'd need to manually update investment values.

**Winner: Simplifi** for net worth tracking.

---

### Cost Over Time

Simplifi at $47.88/year is genuinely affordable for a budgeting app. Over 5 years, that's ~$240.

Notion's free plan handles basic budgeting for most people. The Plus plan ($10/month) is overkill for budgeting unless you're also using Notion for other work.

**Winner: Notion** — especially if you stick to the free plan.

## Who Should Use Simplifi?

- You want **automatic transaction tracking** without manual data entry
- You're **switching away from Mint** and want something similar but better-designed
- You care more about **time savings than deep customization**
- You want investment account and net worth tracking in the same app
- You're **new to budgeting** and want something that works out of the box

---

## Who Should Use Notion?

- You want **complete control** over your budget structure and design
- You're already using **Notion for other areas of your life** (tasks, projects, notes)
- You're comfortable with **manual data entry** or semi-manual CSV imports
- You care about **data privacy** and don't want to share bank credentials
- You want to build a **custom budgeting method** that no app supports natively
- You're **self-employed or freelance** with complex income and expense tracking needs

## The Bottom Line

**Choose Simplifi if** you want a set-it-and-forget-it budgeting app that tracks your spending automatically and shows you where your money is going with minimal effort. It's particularly well-suited for W-2 employees with predictable income.

**Choose Notion if** you want to build a budget that perfectly fits your financial life, you're already in the Notion ecosystem, or you're self-employed with complex tracking needs.

Neither is objectively better — they serve different types of budgeters.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Simplifi worth the money?

At $3.99/month ($47.99/year), Simplifi is one of the more affordable budgeting apps with automatic bank sync. If you value automatic transaction tracking and clean spending reports and would otherwise spend 30+ minutes a month managing your budget manually, the cost is easily justified. If you're comfortable with manual tracking, Notion (free) achieves similar results for $0.

### Can Notion replace a budgeting app like Simplifi?

Yes — for the right person. Notion can replicate most budgeting functions (expense tracking, savings goals, category summaries) through database views. The key difference is automation: Simplifi imports transactions automatically; Notion requires manual entry or CSV imports. If you don't mind the manual step, Notion is a powerful and free alternative.

### Which is better for self-employed budgeting?

Notion, in most cases. Self-employed budgeting involves irregular income, multiple income streams, tax set-asides, and business expense tracking. Notion's flexible database structure handles these complexities well, while Simplifi's structured spending plans work best for regular W-2 income.

### Does Simplifi connect to all banks?

Simplifi connects to thousands of financial institutions via Plaid. However, some smaller credit unions and regional banks aren't supported. Before subscribing, check if your specific accounts are compatible using Simplifi's free trial.

### Can I use both Simplifi and Notion together?

Yes. Some people use Simplifi for automatic transaction tracking and reporting, then export data to Notion for deeper analysis, annual planning, or goal tracking. The combination is overkill for most users, but it works well if you want automation and customization simultaneously.
