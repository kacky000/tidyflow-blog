## The 7 Best Expense Tracking Apps for Small Business

### 1. QuickBooks Online — Best Overall

**Price:** $30/mo (Simple Start) · $60/mo (Essentials) · $90/mo (Plus) · $200/mo (Advanced)

QuickBooks Online is the default choice for a reason: it connects bookkeeping, invoicing, payroll, and expense tracking in one place. The mobile app lets you photograph receipts and auto-categorize them using machine learning that improves based on your past entries.

The mileage tracker runs in the background on your phone and logs trips automatically — no manual entry. At tax time, you export a categorized expense report directly to your accountant or TurboTax.

**Key strength:** Accountant ecosystem. Nearly every CPA and bookkeeper knows QuickBooks, which means less friction when handing off your books.

**Key weakness:** Price creep. The $30/mo Simple Start plan limits you to one user and locks out key features. Most small businesses end up on the $60–$90/mo plans. Add payroll ($45+/mo) and the bill climbs fast.

**Best for:** Businesses with 2–15 employees, complex expenses, or plans to hire an accountant.

---

### 2. FreshBooks — Best for Service-Based Businesses

**Price:** $19/mo (Lite) · $33/mo (Plus) · $60/mo (Premium)

FreshBooks started as an invoicing tool and built expense tracking around it. That lineage shows — it handles time tracking, project-based billing, and client invoicing better than any other option on this list. When you log an expense against a project, you can bill it back to the client in two clicks.

Receipt scanning works reliably. You forward email receipts to a dedicated FreshBooks address, and the system logs them automatically. The mobile app OCR is accurate on clear receipts but occasionally misreads amounts on crumpled or low-contrast images.

**Key strength:** Time-to-invoice workflow. If you bill clients by the hour or by project, FreshBooks makes the expense → invoice → payment chain faster than QuickBooks.

**Key weakness:** Team expense reports are limited. FreshBooks isn't designed for employees submitting expense reports for reimbursement — that workflow is clunky compared to Expensify or Zoho Expense.

**Best for:** Consultants, agencies, freelancers with a small team, and service businesses that bill clients directly for expenses.

---

### 3. Expensify — Best for Receipt Scanning and Team Expense Reports

**Price:** $5/user/mo (Collect) · $9/user/mo (Control) · Free for individuals (limited)

Expensify's SmartScan technology is genuinely the best receipt OCR on the market. Point your camera at a receipt, and within seconds it extracts the merchant, date, amount, and category. On high-volume months, you can submit 50+ receipts without touching a keyboard.

The team expense report workflow is where Expensify earns its price. Employees submit reports on mobile; managers approve with one tap; finance exports to QuickBooks, Xero, or NetSuite. The approval chain is configurable — you can require manager approval over $100 and auto-approve anything under $25.

Corporate card reconciliation is a standout feature: connect company cards, and Expensify matches card charges to submitted receipts automatically, flagging duplicates or gaps.

**Key strength:** Receipt scanning accuracy and team reimbursement flow. If your team travels and submits expenses regularly, nothing else comes close.

**Key weakness:** Expensify is an expense management layer, not a full accounting system. You still need QuickBooks, Xero, or similar for your books — Expensify integrates with them but doesn't replace them.

**Best for:** Businesses with 3–50 employees who travel, attend events, or have distributed teams submitting regular expense reports.

---

### 4. Zoho Expense — Best Budget Option

**Price:** Free (up to 3 users) · $3/user/mo (Standard) · $5/user/mo (Premium) · $8/user/mo (Enterprise)

Zoho Expense delivers more features per dollar than any other option here. At $3/user/mo, you get receipt scanning, multi-currency support, approval workflows, mileage tracking, and integrations with QuickBooks and Xero. The free tier covers up to 3 users with 5GB storage — enough for a solo founder with one or two contractors.

If you already use Zoho Books, Zoho CRM, or other Zoho products, Expense fits in natively and data flows automatically between apps without any integration work.

Receipt scanning quality is solid but a half-step below Expensify's SmartScan. On clear receipts, accuracy is excellent. On faded or folded receipts, you'll find yourself correcting more entries.

**Key strength:** Price-to-feature ratio and Zoho ecosystem fit. For businesses already on Zoho or those watching costs carefully, it's hard to beat $3/user/mo.

**Key weakness:** The interface has a steeper learning curve than FreshBooks or Expensify. New team members take longer to onboard, and the mobile app has historically been less polished than the web version.

**Best for:** Small teams on tight budgets, businesses already using Zoho products, and international businesses needing multi-currency support without paying premium prices.

---

### 5. Wave — Best Free Option

**Price:** Free (accounting + invoicing) · $16/mo (Pro, adds receipt scanning and bank connections) · Payroll from $20/mo

Wave covers the basics for free: connect your bank accounts, categorize transactions, generate financial reports. If you run a simple operation — one owner, no employees, domestic transactions only — Wave can handle expense tracking without costing anything.

The free tier doesn't include receipt scanning OCR; you photograph receipts but manually enter amounts. The Wave Pro plan at $16/mo adds automated receipt scanning and better bank feeds, which most real businesses need.

Wave's reporting is functional but basic. You can pull an expense report by category, but you won't get the multi-dimensional analysis (by project, by client, by team member) that QuickBooks or Xero offer.

**Key strength:** It's free. For a brand-new business watching every dollar, Wave lets you start tracking immediately with zero commitment.

**Key weakness:** Wave has limited scalability. Once you add employees, handle complex project accounting, or need multi-currency support, you'll outgrow it. Migrating away is painful — export formats are limited.

**Best for:** Solo founders, very early-stage businesses with simple finances, and businesses that need basic bookkeeping without paying for it.

---

### 6. Xero — Best for International Businesses

**Price:** $29/mo (Early) · $46/mo (Growing) · $62/mo (Established)

Xero is the strongest accounting platform for businesses operating across borders. It handles 160+ currencies natively, auto-converts at real exchange rates, and manages VAT/GST calculations for multiple tax jurisdictions out of the box. If you pay vendors in euros, invoice clients in pounds, and run your books in USD, Xero handles that without workarounds.

The expense tracking features are solid across all plans: receipt capture via mobile, mileage tracking, employee expense claims, and reimbursement tracking. The Xero app marketplace connects to 1,000+ third-party tools, giving it the widest integration breadth on this list.

**Key strength:** Multi-currency and international tax handling. No other tool in this price range comes close for businesses operating in multiple countries.

**Key weakness:** Xero's US market share is smaller than QuickBooks, which means fewer local accountants and bookkeepers are familiar with it. If you plan to work with a US-based CPA, verify they're comfortable in Xero before committing.

**Best for:** E-commerce businesses with international suppliers, agencies with overseas clients, and any business billing or paying in multiple currencies.

---

### 7. Ramp — Best for Businesses Using Corporate Cards

**Price:** Free (core platform with Ramp card) · $15/user/mo (Ramp Plus) · Custom (Enterprise)

Ramp flips the expense tracking model: instead of tracking what employees spend and reimbursing them later, Ramp issues corporate cards and captures expenses at the point of purchase. Every swipe auto-generates a receipt request to the cardholder via Slack or text. They snap a photo, attach a memo, and the expense is categorized and logged.

The result is near-zero expense report backlog. Finance teams running Ramp report 80–90% fewer manual entries compared to traditional reimbursement workflows.

Ramp also runs AI-powered spend analysis that flags duplicate subscriptions, vendor price anomalies, and categories where spending is trending above historical norms. The savings feature has identified real cost reductions for businesses — duplicate software subscriptions alone often cover the platform's cost.

**Key strength:** Eliminates the reimbursement cycle entirely. If the pain point is employees fronting personal money and submitting reports, Ramp removes that workflow.

**Key weakness:** Ramp requires issuing Ramp corporate cards to your team. If employees need to use personal cards (travel bookings, international vendors), you're back to a manual process for those expenses. Also, Ramp works best for US-based businesses — international support is limited.

**Best for:** Businesses with 5+ employees making regular purchases, any business spending $50K+/year on controllable expenses, and teams where expense report management is a real time drain.

## How to Choose the Right Expense Tracker

**You're a solo operator or early-stage:** Start with Wave (free) or FreshBooks ($19/mo if you invoice clients). Don't over-engineer it.

**Your team submits expense reports regularly:** Expensify ($5/user/mo) or Zoho Expense ($3/user/mo). Expensify has better receipt scanning; Zoho has better pricing.

**You want everything in one platform:** QuickBooks Online handles accounting, payroll, and expenses in one place. It's the most expensive option but reduces software fragmentation.

**You operate internationally:** Xero is the clear choice. Multi-currency handling and VAT/GST support justify the price for any business billing across borders.

**Your team uses corporate cards:** Ramp eliminates expense reports almost entirely and is free with the card. The ROI calculation is simple — if you spend time chasing receipts and processing reimbursements, Ramp pays for itself quickly.

## FAQ

**Do I need a separate expense tracker if I already have accounting software?**

Not always. QuickBooks, FreshBooks, and Xero include expense tracking as part of their accounting platforms. You only need a standalone tool like Expensify or Zoho Expense if your team submits expense reports for reimbursement and your accounting software handles that workflow poorly.

**Can expense tracking apps import bank and credit card transactions automatically?**

Yes — all seven apps on this list connect to US bank accounts and credit cards via bank feeds. Transactions import automatically, and the app categorizes them based on merchant type and your historical patterns. You review and confirm, rather than entering manually.

**How much time does expense tracking software actually save?**

Studies vary, but the typical small business saves 3–6 hours per month on manual data entry and receipt organization after switching to an automated app. The bigger savings come at tax time — clean, categorized records reduce the time your accountant spends on your books, which directly reduces your accounting fees.

**Is the data secure?**

All major expense tracking apps use bank-level 256-bit SSL encryption and store data on secure servers. They connect to banks via read-only access (they can see transactions but cannot move money). Check each provider's security page and compliance certifications if you're in a regulated industry.

**What happens if my business outgrows the app I choose?**

Most apps export data in CSV or QBO format, which accounting software can import. The painful part is re-training your team and recategorizing historical data. That's a real cost — which is why it's worth choosing a tool with room to grow, rather than starting with Wave and migrating to QuickBooks six months later.

## Verdict

For most small businesses with a team, **QuickBooks Online** is the safest default — broad accountant support, deep integrations, and a feature set that covers the next several years of growth. The price is real, but the ecosystem value justifies it.

If your team submits expense reports regularly, **Expensify** or **Zoho Expense** handles that workflow better than any accounting platform at a fraction of the per-user cost — and both integrate cleanly with QuickBooks or Xero for your books.

If you're truly early-stage and watching cash flow, **Wave** gets you started for free. Just be realistic about migration costs when you outgrow it.

And if corporate card control is what you're after — cutting out the manual reimbursement cycle entirely — **Ramp** is worth a serious look before defaulting to any of the others.
