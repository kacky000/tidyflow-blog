# Splitwise vs Venmo: Which Should You Use to Split Bills in 2026?

**Splitwise vs Venmo** is the question almost every roommate household and friend group eventually asks. They sound like competitors, but they actually solve two different halves of the same problem. Splitwise *tracks* who owes what; Venmo *moves* the money. Understanding that split is the key to choosing — or, for many people, using both.

This guide compares Splitwise and Venmo on tracking, payments, fees, group features, and the situations where each one wins. It is a neutral, practical comparison — no investment or financial advice. ## The Core Difference: Ledger vs Wallet

The single most important thing to understand is this: **Splitwise is a ledger, Venmo is a wallet.**

- **Splitwise** records every shared expense and keeps a running tally of who owes whom. It does not actually transfer money — at settle-up time it tells you the net amounts and links out to a payment app. Its value is *clarity over time*.
- **Venmo** is built to *move money now*. You can request and pay in seconds, and it has light splitting and Groups features, but its tracking is shallow compared to a dedicated ledger.

If your shared costs are simple and you settle each one immediately, Venmo alone is fine. If balances build up across a month — rent, utilities, groceries, the streaming bundle — Splitwise keeps everyone honest in a way Venmo can't.

## Tracking and Splitting: Splitwise Wins Clearly

Splitwise is purpose-built for splitting. You can divide a bill **equally, by exact dollar amounts, by percentage, or by shares**, which is essential when roommates have different room sizes or incomes. It remembers recurring expenses, shows each person's net balance at a glance, and simplifies debts so the group settles with the fewest possible transfers.

Venmo can split a single charge among several people and offers Groups for a shared tab, but it does not maintain a clean, simplified, long-running ledger the way Splitwise does. For *anything beyond a simple even split settled on the spot*, Splitwise is the better tracker. ## Payments and Speed: Venmo Wins Clearly

This is Venmo's home turf. Splitwise tells you that your roommate owes you $214 — but you still have to *get* the money, and Splitwise doesn't move it. Venmo does, instantly, with a tap. Standard transfers funded by your Venmo balance or a linked bank account are free.

Venmo also has the social/convenience layer many people already live in: pay by username, add a note, and settle a tab from your phone in seconds. If everyone in your group already has Venmo, settling up is effortless. The catch is fees, covered next. ## Fees: Read the Fine Print

**Splitwise** is free for its core tracking. It offers an optional **Pro tier** with extras (receipt scanning, charts, currency conversion), but the free version covers what most households need. Since Splitwise doesn't move money, it has no transfer fees.

**Venmo** is free on standard transfers from your balance or a linked bank account, but watch two charges:

- **Instant transfer to your bank** carries a percentage fee.
- **Paying with a credit card** adds a surcharge (around 3%).

Keep Venmo free by using bank/debit funding and standard (not instant) withdrawals. To see how even small percentage fees add up across many settle-ups, run the numbers in our [bill split calculator](/tools/bill-split-calculator/).

## When to Use Splitwise

Choose Splitwise (as your tracker) when you:

- Share ongoing costs that build up over a month (rent, utilities, groceries)
- Need uneven splits by amount, percentage, or shares
- Want one clean net balance instead of dozens of small payments
- Live with roommates long-term and want a permanent record
- Want to *minimize* the number of transfers at settle-up

---

## When to Use Venmo

Choose Venmo (as your wallet) when you:

- Need to pay or get paid instantly
- Mostly settle one-off charges on the spot
- Already have everyone in the group on Venmo
- Want payment + light splitting in a single app
- Don't carry complex balances across the month

## The Honest Verdict: Use Both

For most roommates and friend groups, **Splitwise vs Venmo is a false choice** — the best setup is **Splitwise to track, Venmo to settle**. Log shared expenses in Splitwise all month so the ledger is fair and transparent, then use Venmo to pay the single net balance when it's time to settle up.

If your situation is genuinely simple — even splits, paid immediately — skip the ledger and just use Venmo. If your situation is complex or money is tight and you want to avoid every fee, track in a free spreadsheet and settle with a no-fee rail like a bank transfer. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Splitwise move money like Venmo?

No. Splitwise only *tracks* who owes what — it does not transfer funds. At settle-up it shows the net amounts and links out to a payment app. Venmo actually moves the money. That's why many people use Splitwise to track and Venmo to pay.

### Is Splitwise free?

Yes, Splitwise's core expense tracking and balance features are free, including uneven splits. It offers an optional Pro tier with extras like receipt scanning and charts, but most households are fine on the free version.

### Does Venmo charge fees to split bills?

Standard Venmo transfers from your balance or linked bank account are free. Venmo charges for instant transfers to your bank and adds a surcharge (around 3%) for credit-card payments. Use bank/debit funding and standard withdrawals to avoid fees.

### Which is better for roommates, Splitwise or Venmo?

For ongoing roommate expenses, use both: Splitwise to track running balances and uneven splits, Venmo to settle the net amount. Splitwise alone can't move money; Venmo alone struggles with complex, long-running balances.
