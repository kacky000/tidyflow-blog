## Individual Card Reviews

### 1. Blue Cash Everyday from American Express

**Grocery cash back:** 3% at U.S. supermarkets (on up to $6,000 per year in purchases, then 1%)

The Blue Cash Everyday is often the first card that comes up in grocery rewards discussions, and for good reason. A flat 3% back at U.S. supermarkets with no activation or category rotation makes it one of the most straightforward options available. If your household spends $500/month on groceries, that translates to $180 per year in cash back from groceries alone.

Beyond the supermarket, you also earn 3% on U.S. online retail purchases and 3% at U.S. gas stations, making it a solid everyday carry. The $200 welcome offer after spending $2,000 in the first 3 months is reasonable for regular household spending.

**Watch out for:** The $6,000 annual cap on the 3% grocery rate. Larger families who spend more than $500/month at the supermarket will hit that ceiling before December. Also, Amex acceptance is slightly less universal than Visa/Mastercard at smaller grocery stores and warehouse clubs like Costco.

**Estimated annual grocery savings (at $600/mo spend):** $180 (3% on first $6,000) + $12 (1% on remaining $1,200) = $192

### 2. Chase Freedom Flex

**Grocery cash back:** 5% on up to $1,500 per quarter (when groceries are the activated rotating category)

The Chase Freedom Flex earns 5% on rotating quarterly categories that you need to activate each quarter. Groceries typically appear as a bonus category once or twice per year. During those quarters, $1,500 in grocery spending earns $75 — which is excellent, but unpredictable.

Outside of rotating categories, the card earns 3% on dining and drugstores, 5% on travel purchased through Chase Travel, and 1% on everything else. This makes it a strong secondary card for months when groceries aren't in the bonus rotation.

The $200 welcome bonus after just $500 in spending within 3 months is one of the lowest thresholds on this list, which is a plus for anyone who prefers a low commitment to unlock the bonus.

**Watch out for:** You must manually activate the quarterly bonus category each quarter, and groceries aren't always included. This card works best when paired with a dedicated grocery card like the Blue Cash Everyday.

**Estimated annual grocery savings (at $600/mo spend):** Varies. In a quarter with grocery bonus: $75. In other quarters: $18 (1%). Full-year estimate: ~$129 (assuming grocery category active 2 of 4 quarters).

### 3. Citi Custom Cash

**Grocery cash back:** Up to 5% on your top eligible spending category each billing cycle (up to $500 in purchases per cycle)

The Citi Custom Cash automatically applies 5% cash back to whichever eligible category you spend the most in each billing cycle — no activation needed. If groceries are consistently your biggest monthly expense (and for most households, they are), you earn 5% automatically.

The key constraint is the $500 per billing cycle cap. Spending beyond $500 in your top category earns 1%. For individuals or couples with a monthly grocery bill under $500, this card delivers the highest effective grocery rate on this list. For larger families spending $800+, the cap limits its value.

The $200 welcome bonus requires $1,500 in spending over 6 months — a generous timeline.

**Watch out for:** The $500/month cap is firm. If your grocery bill exceeds $500, the effective rate drops significantly. Also, if another category (say, gas or dining) accidentally exceeds your grocery spend in a given month, the 5% shifts to that category instead.

**Estimated annual grocery savings (at $600/mo spend):** $300 (5% on first $500/mo) + $12 (1% on remaining $100/mo) = $312

### 4. Capital One SavorOne Cash Rewards

**Grocery cash back:** 3% at grocery stores

The SavorOne is a lifestyle rewards card that gives 3% back on groceries, dining, entertainment, popular streaming services, and 5% on travel booked through Capital One Travel. There are no spending caps on the 3% grocery category, which sets it apart from the Blue Cash Everyday.

For households that spend heavily across dining, entertainment, and groceries, the SavorOne provides consistent 3% earnings across several everyday categories without needing to track rotating bonuses or spending caps.

The $200 welcome bonus requires $500 in spending within 3 months. The card also has no foreign transaction fees, which is a nice perk for travelers.

**Watch out for:** The 3% grocery rate is competitive but not the highest on this list. If groceries are your only priority, the Citi Custom Cash (5%) or Blue Cash Everyday (3% with additional perks) may return more. The SavorOne's strength is breadth, not depth.

**Estimated annual grocery savings (at $600/mo spend):** $216 (3% on all $7,200, no cap)

### 5. Wells Fargo Active Cash

**Grocery cash back:** 2% on all purchases (including groceries)

The Active Cash doesn't have a grocery bonus category — it pays a flat 2% on every purchase, period. No categories to track, no caps, no activation. For people who don't want to think about which card to use at which store, this is the simplest option.

While 2% on groceries is lower than the other cards on this list, the Active Cash eliminates decision fatigue entirely. It also means you earn 2% on every non-grocery purchase that would otherwise earn 1% on a category-specific card.

The $200 welcome bonus after $500 in the first 3 months matches the lowest thresholds on this list.

**Watch out for:** You're leaving money on the table at the grocery store compared to cards that offer 3%-5%. The Active Cash is ideal as a "default" card for non-bonus purchases, paired with a dedicated grocery card.

**Estimated annual grocery savings (at $600/mo spend):** $144 (flat 2% on $7,200)

## How We Chose These Cards

Every card on this list meets four criteria:

1. **$0 annual fee** — No card on this list charges an annual fee. The savings from cash back should not be offset by fees.
2. **Meaningful grocery rewards** — Each card returns at least 2% on grocery purchases, either through a dedicated grocery category or a flat-rate structure.
3. **Wide availability** — All five cards are issued by major U.S. banks and are available to applicants with good to excellent credit (generally 670+ FICO).
4. **No deceptive structures** — We excluded cards with introductory rates that drop dramatically after year one, or cards with rewards that can only be redeemed in narrow, low-value ways.

We did not consider cards with annual fees (even "waived first year" offers), store-branded cards limited to a single retailer, or cards that require a specific bank account to unlock their full rewards rate.

## Tips for Maximizing Grocery Rewards

**Pair two cards.** The most effective strategy is pairing a high-rate grocery card (like the Citi Custom Cash at 5%) with a flat-rate card (like the Active Cash at 2%) for everything else. This way, you maximize grocery returns without sacrificing rewards on non-grocery spending.

**Know what counts as "groceries."** Card issuers use merchant category codes (MCCs) to classify purchases. Supermarkets and grocery stores generally qualify, but warehouse clubs (Costco, Sam's Club), big-box stores (Target, Walmart), and convenience stores often do not. Check your card's terms for specifics.

**Track your spending against caps.** If your card has a spending cap on the bonus rate (like the Blue Cash Everyday's $6,000/year or the Citi Custom Cash's $500/month), monitor your spending so you can switch to a flat-rate card once you've maxed out the bonus tier.

**Use a grocery budget.** Earning cash back does not help if you're overspending at the store. Building a clear grocery budget — and sticking to it — is what actually saves money. **Pay your balance in full every month.** This is non-negotiable. Credit card interest rates typically range from 20% to 30% APR. Carrying a balance of even a few hundred dollars wipes out months of cash back earnings. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do warehouse clubs like Costco and Sam's Club count as "grocery" purchases?

Generally, no. Most card issuers classify warehouse clubs under their own merchant category code, which is separate from supermarkets and grocery stores. The Blue Cash Everyday, Citi Custom Cash, and Capital One SavorOne typically do not award grocery bonus rates at warehouse clubs. Always check your card's specific terms.

### Can I use more than one of these cards at the same time?

Yes. Many people use a dedicated grocery card for supermarket purchases and a flat-rate card (like the Wells Fargo Active Cash) for everything else. There is no rule against holding multiple no-annual-fee cards, and doing so is a common strategy to maximize total cash back.

### What happens when I hit the spending cap on a grocery bonus category?

Once you exceed the cap (e.g., $6,000/year on Blue Cash Everyday or $500/month on Citi Custom Cash), additional grocery purchases earn the card's base rate — usually 1%. At that point, switching to a flat 2% card for the remainder of the period gives you a better return.

### Is the Citi Custom Cash really 5% on groceries automatically?

Yes, but with a condition. The 5% applies to whichever eligible category has the highest spend in each billing cycle, up to $500. If groceries are consistently your top category, you'll earn 5% on grocery purchases automatically. But if you spend more on gas or dining in a given month, the 5% shifts to that category instead.

### Do any of these cards charge foreign transaction fees?

The Capital One SavorOne and Chase Freedom Flex do not charge foreign transaction fees. The Blue Cash Everyday, Citi Custom Cash, and Wells Fargo Active Cash do charge a 3% foreign transaction fee. This is only relevant if you shop at international online retailers or travel abroad.

## Verdict

For most households, the **Citi Custom Cash** delivers the highest grocery return at 5% — as long as your monthly grocery bill stays under $500 and groceries remain your top spending category. For families spending $500-$1,000/month on groceries, the **Blue Cash Everyday** offers a reliable 3% with a higher annual cap and no need to worry about category shifting.

If you prefer zero complexity, the **Wells Fargo Active Cash** at a flat 2% on everything is hard to beat for simplicity, though you'll earn less at the supermarket. And if your spending is spread across groceries, dining, and entertainment fairly evenly, the **Capital One SavorOne** gives you 3% across all of those without caps.

The best approach for most people is to pair a high-rate grocery card with a flat-rate card for non-grocery purchases. That combination typically returns $300-$500 per year without any annual fees — real money that compounds over time when directed into savings or debt repayment.
