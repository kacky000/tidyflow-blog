# EveryDollar Pricing 2026: Plans, Fees, and Is It Worth It?

**EveryDollar pricing** in 2026 comes down to a simple choice: use the free plan with manual transaction entry, or pay $79.99 per year for Premium and get automatic bank syncing. Built by Ramsey Solutions — Dave Ramsey's company — EveryDollar is a [zero-based budgeting](/blog/zero-based-budgeting-guide/) app where every dollar of income gets assigned to a specific category before the month begins. No investing features, no credit monitoring, no financial product upsells. Just budgeting.

That simplicity is the point. But whether the free plan is enough or Premium justifies its cost depends on how you budget and how much friction you can tolerate. This guide covers exactly what each tier costs, what you get, what you do not get, and how EveryDollar stacks up against alternatives.

---

## EveryDollar Pricing at a Glance

| Plan | Cost | Effective Monthly | Key Features |
|------|------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Free** | $0 | $0 | Manual transaction entry, unlimited categories, basic budget reports |
| **Premium (Monthly)** | $17.99/month | $17.99 | Bank connection, custom reports, priority support |
| **Premium (Annual)** | $79.99/year | ~$6.67 | Same as monthly Premium, billed once per year |

There is no trial period for Premium. You either use the free plan or pay. No middle ground, no time-limited unlock.

---

## The Free Plan: What You Actually Get

EveryDollar's free tier is a functional zero-based budgeting tool. You can create unlimited budget categories, set spending targets, and track your progress throughout the month. Here is what is included:

- **Unlimited budget categories** — no cap on how many line items you create (groceries, rent, gas, subscriptions, giving, debt payments, etc.)
- **Manual transaction entry** — you log every purchase yourself, either by typing it in or dragging items between categories
- **Basic spending reports** — see month-to-month totals, though the visualization is limited compared to Premium
- **Debt payoff tracking** — built-in debt snowball tracker, consistent with Ramsey's debt elimination method
- **Multi-device access** — use EveryDollar on your phone, tablet, and desktop browser

The free plan does not connect to your bank. Every single transaction must be entered by hand. For some people, this is actually a feature — manual entry forces awareness. For others, it is a dealbreaker that leads to abandoned budgets within weeks.

The free plan also lacks custom reporting. You get a basic overview of spending by category, but you cannot generate reports filtered by date range, compare months side by side, or export data.

---

## EveryDollar Premium: What $79.99/Year Gets You

Premium (previously called "EveryDollar Plus") adds the features that make budgeting less labor-intensive:

### Bank Connection (Auto-Import Transactions)

This is the headline feature. Premium connects to your checking accounts, savings accounts, and credit cards, then automatically imports transactions into your budget. You still need to drag each transaction into the correct category — EveryDollar does not auto-categorize like some competitors — but you no longer need to type amounts and merchant names manually.

For someone with 40-80 transactions per month, this alone saves 15-30 minutes of data entry weekly.

### Custom Reports

Premium unlocks detailed spending reports that let you:

- Compare spending across multiple months
- Filter by specific categories or date ranges
- See trends in your spending behavior over time
- Identify categories where you consistently overspend

### Priority Support

Free users get standard email support. Premium subscribers get faster response times and priority handling. In practice, EveryDollar's support needs are minimal — it is a simple app — but if you hit a bank connection issue, priority access matters.

### Monthly vs. Annual Premium Pricing

If you choose Premium, the annual plan is significantly cheaper:

- **Monthly:** $17.99/month = $215.88/year
- **Annual:** $79.99/year = ~$6.67/month

Paying monthly costs nearly 2.7 times more than annual billing. Unless you are genuinely unsure whether you will stick with Premium for more than four months, the annual plan is the only rational choice. Four months of monthly billing ($71.96) already approaches the full annual price.

## Are There Hidden Fees?

No. EveryDollar's pricing is straightforward:

- **No per-account connection fees** — connect as many bank accounts as you want on Premium
- **No setup fees** — the app is free to download, and Premium activates immediately
- **No cancellation penalties** — cancel anytime; you retain access until the end of your billing period
- **No family surcharge** — share access with a spouse or partner at no extra cost
- **No data export fees** — Premium includes CSV export as part of the subscription

The one caveat: if you are deep in the Ramsey ecosystem, EveryDollar Premium is bundled with Ramsey+ ($129.99/year), which includes Financial Peace University and other Ramsey courses. Buying Premium standalone at $79.99 is cheaper if you only want the budgeting app. But if you also want the courses, Ramsey+ offers better combined value.

## How EveryDollar Compares on Price

EveryDollar sits in the middle of the budgeting app market. Here is how it stacks up:

| App | Free Plan | Paid Plan | Annual Cost | Method |
|-----|-----------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **EveryDollar** | Yes (manual entry) | Premium | $79.99/year | Zero-based |
| **YNAB** | No (34-day trial only) | Single tier | $99/year | Zero-based |
| **Goodbudget** | Yes (10 envelopes) | Plus | $80/year | Envelope |
| **Monarch Money** | No (7-day trial) | Single tier | $119.88/year | Tracking + budgeting |
| **Spreadsheet** | Yes | — | $0 | Manual |

A few observations:

**EveryDollar vs. YNAB:** YNAB costs $99/year with no free tier, making EveryDollar cheaper at $79.99 — and EveryDollar offers a genuinely usable free plan. However, YNAB auto-categorizes transactions and has a more mature rule-based system. EveryDollar requires you to manually drag transactions into categories even on Premium. For a deeper look, see our [YNAB pricing breakdown](/blog/ynab-pricing-2026/).

**EveryDollar vs. Goodbudget:** Goodbudget's free tier limits you to 10 envelopes, while EveryDollar's free plan allows unlimited categories. Goodbudget Plus ($80/year) costs nearly the same as EveryDollar Premium ($79.99/year). If you prefer the [envelope budgeting method](/blog/envelope-budgeting-method/), Goodbudget is purpose-built for it. If you want the Ramsey zero-based approach, EveryDollar is the native tool. See our [Goodbudget pricing analysis](/blog/goodbudget-pricing-2026/) or our [Goodbudget vs EveryDollar comparison](/blog/goodbudget-vs-everydollar-2026/) for a detailed breakdown.

**EveryDollar vs. Monarch Money:** Monarch ($9.99/month or $119.88/year) is pricier but includes investment tracking, net worth monitoring, and advanced analytics that EveryDollar does not offer. If you need more than pure budgeting, Monarch is worth the premium. If you only need a budget, EveryDollar costs less.

## Who Should Use the Free Plan

The free plan works well if you:

- Have a simple financial life (one bank account, predictable income, fewer than 30 transactions per month)
- Actually prefer manual entry because it forces you to think about every purchase
- Are paying off debt and want the built-in debt snowball tracker without paying for another subscription
- Want to try zero-based budgeting before committing any money

The free plan is also a reasonable choice for students or anyone on a very tight budget where $79.99 per year genuinely matters.

---

## Who Should Pay for Premium

Premium makes sense if you:

- Have multiple bank accounts and credit cards generating 50+ transactions per month
- Know from experience that manual entry leads to abandoned budgets by week three
- Want spending reports that show trends across months, not just the current period
- Share finances with a partner and need both of you seeing the same synced data in real time

The core question is whether automated transaction import will keep you budgeting consistently. If the answer is yes, $79.99 per year is a small price for sustained financial awareness.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does EveryDollar cost in 2026?

EveryDollar offers a completely free plan with manual transaction entry. Premium costs $79.99 per year (or $17.99/month). The annual plan works out to about $6.67 per month.

### Is the EveryDollar free plan actually usable?

Yes. Unlike many "free" apps that cripple core features, EveryDollar's free tier gives you unlimited budget categories, debt tracking, and multi-device access. The only major limitation is manual transaction entry — no bank syncing.

### What is the difference between EveryDollar Premium and Ramsey+?

EveryDollar Premium ($79.99/year) includes only the budgeting app with bank connection, custom reports, and priority support. Ramsey+ ($129.99/year) bundles EveryDollar Premium with Financial Peace University, the BabySteps app, and other Ramsey courses. If you only want budgeting, standalone Premium is cheaper.

### Does EveryDollar auto-categorize transactions?

No. Even on Premium, EveryDollar imports transactions from your bank but requires you to manually drag each one into the correct budget category. This is a deliberate design choice — Ramsey Solutions wants you to intentionally assign every transaction. Competitors like YNAB and Monarch Money offer auto-categorization with learning rules.

### Is EveryDollar worth it compared to YNAB?

EveryDollar is $20/year cheaper than YNAB ($79.99 vs. $99) and offers a functional free plan that YNAB lacks. However, YNAB has more advanced features: auto-categorization rules, goal tracking, credit card handling, and age-of-money metrics. If you follow Ramsey's system, EveryDollar is the natural fit. If you want the most full-featured zero-based budgeting tool regardless of philosophy, YNAB edges ahead. See our [YNAB pricing guide](/blog/ynab-pricing-2026/) for the full comparison.

### Can I share EveryDollar with my spouse?

Yes. Both the free and Premium plans allow you to share your budget with a partner at no additional cost. You both sign in to the same account and see the same budget in real time.

### Does EveryDollar work for cash envelope budgeting?

EveryDollar is built around zero-based budgeting, not physical cash envelopes. You can mimic envelope categories digitally by creating budget lines for each spending category, but it lacks the explicit envelope metaphor that apps like Goodbudget use. If [envelope budgeting](/blog/envelope-budgeting-method/) is your preferred method, Goodbudget may be a better fit.

## The Verdict

EveryDollar's pricing is fair for what it delivers. The free plan is one of the more generous options in budgeting apps — unlimited categories with no time limit — and Premium at $79.99/year undercuts YNAB while covering the essentials most budgeters need.

The app is best suited for people who follow or are open to Dave Ramsey's financial philosophy: zero-based budgeting, debt snowball, no credit card optimization. If that describes you, EveryDollar is the purpose-built tool for the method, and its price reflects that focus. If you want investment tracking, net worth monitoring, or a budgeting system detached from any particular financial philosophy, look elsewhere.

The free plan is the right starting point for almost everyone. Use it for a full month. If manual entry drives you away, Premium's bank connection is worth the $6.67 per month to keep you budgeting consistently. If you stick with manual entry and it works, you never need to pay at all.
