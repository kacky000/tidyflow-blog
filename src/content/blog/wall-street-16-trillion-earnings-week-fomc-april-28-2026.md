# Wall Street's $16 Trillion Earnings Week: FOMC + Big Tech Could Make or Break the 2026 Rally

The week of April 28 is shaping up to be the most consequential stretch of the year for US financial markets. The Federal Reserve begins its two-day policy meeting on Monday. Alphabet, Meta, and Microsoft report earnings after the closing bell on Tuesday. Amazon and Apple follow on Wednesday. And on Thursday morning, the Commerce Department releases its advance estimate of first-quarter GDP.

The combined market capitalization of the five mega-cap companies reporting this week sits near $16 trillion — roughly a quarter of the entire S&P 500. Their results, taken alongside the Fed's rate decision and GDP data, will determine whether the market's four-week winning streak survives into May or collapses under the weight of its own expectations.

The [S&P 500 closed at 7,165 on Friday, April 25](/blog/sp500-nasdaq-all-time-high-april-2026), marking a fresh all-time high and the fourth consecutive weekly gain. That run has been fueled by a combination of strong earnings, easing geopolitical tensions, and the belief that the Federal Reserve is done tightening. All three assumptions face direct tests in the next five trading sessions.

## FOMC: Every Word in the Statement Matters

The Federal Open Market Committee convenes on Monday, April 28, with the rate decision scheduled for 2:00 p.m. ET on Tuesday, April 29. According to CME FedWatch data, markets assign a 99.7% probability that the committee holds the federal funds rate at 3.50–3.75%, making the decision itself a near-certainty.

The substance of this meeting lies elsewhere. March CPI came in at 3.3% year over year, well above the Fed's 2% target. The labor market has shown no meaningful deterioration. And the April meeting does not include an updated Summary of Economic Projections or dot plot — meaning there will be no fresh rate path guidance from individual committee members. That absence puts extraordinary weight on the post-meeting statement and Chair Jerome Powell's press conference.

Traders will be parsing every word of the statement for any shift in the committee's characterization of inflation progress, economic activity, or the balance of risks. Even minor language changes — replacing "elevated" with "moderating," for instance, or adjusting the forward guidance sentence — could move rate expectations and, by extension, equity valuations.

For a detailed breakdown of the rate decision mechanics and the Kevin Warsh factor, see the [full FOMC April 29 preview](/blog/fed-fomc-april-29-2026-rate-hold-kevin-warsh-preview).

The practical question for markets is not whether rates stay on hold this week — they will — but whether the statement opens or closes the door to cuts later in 2026. With inflation still above target and the economy showing resilience, the Fed has little incentive to signal urgency. But if the statement acknowledges progress on disinflation or flags risks to growth, that would be interpreted as a dovish tilt, potentially extending the equity rally.

## Big Tech Tuesday: Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft

The three mega-cap technology companies reporting on Tuesday collectively represent approximately $9 trillion in market capitalization. Their earnings releases will land within hours of the Fed's rate decision, creating a compressed window in which the market must absorb two of the week's most significant catalysts simultaneously.

**Alphabet** reports after the bell on Tuesday. Wall Street consensus calls for approximately $106.7 billion in revenue and earnings per share near $2.60. The dominant question is whether Google Cloud's AI-driven growth is accelerating fast enough to offset intensifying competition in search advertising. Alphabet's capital expenditure has roughly doubled relative to 2025 levels, and investors want to see that spending reflected in top-line growth — not just capacity buildout. The company's [full Q1 2026 earnings preview](/blog/alphabet-googl-q1-2026-earnings-preview-cloud-ai) details the key metrics to watch across Search, Cloud, and YouTube.

**Meta Platforms** also reports Tuesday. The company disclosed full-year 2026 capital expenditure guidance of $115–135 billion, a figure that drew sharp reactions from analysts when it was announced. That number reflects Meta's aggressive push into AI infrastructure and its continued investment in Reality Labs, the VR/AR division that has consumed tens of billions in cumulative losses. The Q1 results will test whether AI-enhanced advertising — specifically the Advantage+ platform — is generating sufficient revenue growth to justify the magnitude of that spending commitment.

**Microsoft** rounds out the Tuesday trio. Azure cloud revenue growth and the commercial traction of Microsoft 365 Copilot are the two focal points. Microsoft has the clearest AI monetization story among the hyperscalers: GitHub Copilot, Azure OpenAI services, and the broader Copilot ecosystem are generating measurable incremental revenue. Whether that revenue is scaling at a rate that justifies Microsoft's own elevated capex is the question investors need answered. The [Microsoft Q3 FY2026 earnings preview](/blog/microsoft-q3-fy2026-earnings-preview-azure-copilot-april-29) breaks down the Azure, Copilot, and gaming segment expectations in detail.

For a broader look at how these three companies' results interact and what the cross-company themes mean for the sector, the [Big Tech Q1 2026 earnings preview](/blog/big-tech-q1-2026-earnings-preview-alphabet-meta-microsoft) covers the AI capex question across all three names.

## Big Tech Wednesday: Amazon and Apple

The second wave arrives on Wednesday, April 30, when Amazon and Apple report after the close.

**Amazon** enters the week with its cloud division, Amazon Web Services, under particular scrutiny. AWS has been the market share leader in cloud infrastructure for years, but its growth rate has been under pressure from Azure's AI-driven acceleration. Amazon's own AI investments — including a $4 billion commitment to Anthropic and its custom Trainium chip development — are beginning to generate revenue, but the scale of that contribution relative to the overall AWS business remains an open question. Retail margins and the trajectory of the advertising business, which has become Amazon's highest-margin revenue stream, will also receive attention.

**Apple** faces a different set of challenges. The company's supply chain exposure to China and its accelerating production shift toward India have been recurring themes throughout 2026, as tariff policy has introduced uncertainty into hardware margins. Services revenue — the App Store, iCloud, Apple Music, Apple TV+ — has been the growth driver that investors are counting on to sustain premium valuations even as iPhone unit growth moderates. For a detailed look at Apple's tariff exposure, India strategy, and Services trajectory, see the [Apple Q2 2026 earnings preview](/blog/apple-q2-2026-earnings-preview-tariffs-india-services-april-30).

## The $16 Trillion AI Capex Question

Across all five companies, one theme dominates: artificial intelligence spending. The combined capital expenditure commitments of Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, and Apple for 2026 are projected to exceed $350 billion, the largest coordinated infrastructure buildout in the history of the technology industry. The question hanging over earnings season is whether that spending is producing revenue — or whether it is building capacity that will take years to monetize.

The market has been willing to extend patience. Stock prices for all five companies sit at or near record highs, reflecting a consensus view that AI infrastructure investment will eventually translate into durable revenue streams. But patience has limits, and the Q1 results represent the most comprehensive test yet of whether that translation is happening at the rate investors have priced in.

Alphabet's capex roughly doubling year over year. Meta's $115–135 billion full-year commitment. Microsoft's aggressive Azure expansion. Amazon's Anthropic partnership and custom chip development. These are not incremental bets — they are transformational capital allocation decisions that will define each company's financial trajectory for the next decade. The Q1 reports will reveal whether the early returns justify the scale of the wager.

## Earnings Season Scorecard So Far

The broader Q1 2026 earnings season has been solid by historical standards. Approximately 80–85% of S&P 500 companies that have reported so far have beaten analyst estimates, and aggregate profit growth is tracking in the range of 13–16% year over year. That is a healthy showing, though not exceptional enough to prevent selective selling in names that miss or guide lower.

One standout from the past week was Intel, which surged 23% on Friday, April 25, after reporting better-than-expected results driven by a resurgence in data center revenue. The move lifted the entire semiconductor sector and added momentum to the broader market heading into this week. The [Intel Q1 2026 earnings breakdown](/blog/intel-q1-2026-earnings-ai-data-center-surge-april-25) details the data center surge and what it signals for the chip sector.

The Intel result carries a specific implication for this week's Big Tech earnings: it suggests that enterprise and hyperscaler demand for computing infrastructure remained robust through March. If that demand shows up in Azure, AWS, and Google Cloud revenue — and in the forward guidance those companies provide — it would reinforce the narrative that AI spending is translating into real economic activity, not just chip orders.

## GDP: The Macro Backstop

The Commerce Department's advance estimate of first-quarter GDP lands at 8:30 a.m. ET on Wednesday, April 30 — hours before Amazon and Apple report. The number will provide the first official read on whether the US economy maintained its growth trajectory through the January-to-March period, or whether the cumulative effect of elevated interest rates, tariff uncertainty, and sticky inflation began to bite.

Consensus estimates have been gradually revised downward in recent weeks, reflecting softer-than-expected consumer spending data and manufacturing surveys. A GDP print near or above 2% annualized would likely be received neutrally by markets — consistent with a soft landing scenario. A number materially below that threshold could rattle investors, particularly given the equity market's elevated valuations.

The timing creates a sequencing risk: if GDP disappoints on Wednesday morning, it could color the market's reaction to Amazon and Apple earnings that evening, even if those results are objectively solid.

## What Could Go Wrong

The market is priced for a favorable outcome across multiple simultaneous events. That creates asymmetric risk. Several scenarios could disrupt the current rally:

**A hawkish Fed surprise.** If the FOMC statement introduces language suggesting that rate cuts are off the table for the foreseeable future — or worse, that rate hikes are being discussed — equities would likely sell off sharply. The probability is low, but the impact would be severe given current positioning.

**An AI capex backlash.** If one or more of the mega-cap companies signals that AI spending is not generating the expected return on investment, or announces a pause or reduction in capex plans, it would undermine the foundational thesis supporting current valuations. Meta's $115–135 billion guidance number is the most vulnerable to a negative market reaction if the company cannot demonstrate proportional revenue growth.

**Weak forward guidance.** Even if Q1 results beat estimates, conservative guidance for Q2 or full-year 2026 could trigger selling. Companies often manage expectations through cautious guidance, but in an environment where the market is priced for strength, caution gets punished.

**A GDP miss.** A materially weak first-quarter GDP print would raise concerns about the durability of the economic expansion and could shift the market narrative from "soft landing" to "slowdown" — a transition that would not be kind to growth stocks trading at premium multiples.
