# How to Budget for Living in Shreveport, LA

**Budgeting for living in Shreveport, LA** means working within Louisiana's somewhat unusual financial structure — among the lowest property taxes in the country, a reasonable state income tax, and a cost of living significantly below the national average. With a population of 190,000+ in Caddo Parish in Northwest Louisiana, Shreveport is anchored by the gaming industry, medical sector, military employment at Barksdale Air Force Base, and an oil and gas presence that shapes the regional economy.

Here's how to build a realistic monthly budget for Shreveport.

## Budget Breakdown by Income Level

### $2,800/month (Service sector or entry casino employment)
Shreveport's gaming industry employs thousands in dealer, hospitality, security, and food service roles. At $2,800/month take-home, a 1BR apartment at $800, utilities at $140, groceries at $300, and transportation at $220 puts you around $1,900/month — leaving roughly $900 for savings and discretionary spending.

### $3,500/month (Healthcare, government, or skilled trades)
LSU Health Shreveport and Ochsner LSU Health are major employers. At $3,500/month take-home, you can rent a comfortable 2BR, cover all essentials, and consistently save $600-$800/month. This is the sweet spot for single professionals and couples building toward homeownership.

### $4,500-$5,500/month (Military, oil & gas, or medical professional)
Barksdale Air Force Base employs thousands of military and civilian personnel, many with generous benefits packages. Oil and gas workers and medical professionals at this income level in Shreveport are in an excellent financial position — the cost structure allows for aggressive savings, home purchase, and wealth building.

## Shreveport's Unique Cost Factors

### Louisiana Income Tax
Louisiana levies a progressive state income tax at 1.85% (up to $12,500 for single filers) and 4.25% above that. On a $50,000 salary, expect to pay approximately $1,500-$1,800 in Louisiana state income tax per year. This is moderate compared to northeastern states but more than Texas or Florida.

### Property Tax — Extremely Low
This is one of Shreveport's most attractive features for homeowners. Louisiana property taxes average just 0.5-0.7% of assessed value — among the lowest in the entire United States. On a $170,000 home, annual property taxes might be $850-$1,190. This makes homeownership significantly more affordable in Shreveport compared to high-tax states.

### Summer Heat and Humidity
Shreveport summers are hot and humid. Temperatures from June through September regularly reach 95-100°F with high humidity. Air conditioning runs heavily, pushing electric bills to $120-$180/month during peak summer months. Budget for this seasonal spike every year.

### Neighborhood Selection Is Critical
Shreveport has significant variation in safety across neighborhoods. Some areas have elevated crime rates that affect quality of life. Families and professionals typically concentrate in specific areas — research neighborhoods carefully using local crime data before committing to a lease. Established areas near Cross Lake and the South Highlands neighborhood are generally considered stable choices.

### Casino Economy Employment
Shreveport-Bossier City hosts multiple major casinos: Margaritaville Resort Casino, Eldorado Resort Casino, and Hollywood Casino Bossier City, among others. The gaming industry is a significant employer for hospitality, security, food service, and management roles. Tip income and variable shift schedules are common — budget for income variability if you work in this sector.

## What You Save in Shreveport

The combination of low property taxes and below-average rent makes Shreveport one of the more affordable metro areas in the South. Median home prices of $145,000-$195,000 are accessible for working-class and middle-class households, especially with Louisiana's low property tax burden factored in.

Grocery costs are reasonable, and the presence of Walmart, Kroger, ALDI, and regional grocers keeps food prices competitive.

## Major Employers in Shreveport

Understanding Shreveport's job market is essential for budget planning. Major employment sectors include:

- **Healthcare**: LSU Health Shreveport, Ochsner LSU Health, Willis-Knighton Health System
- **Military**: Barksdale Air Force Base (second-largest employer in the metro)
- **Gaming/Hospitality**: Multiple major casinos in Shreveport-Bossier City
- **Energy**: Oil and gas companies with regional operations
- **Education**: Louisiana State University Shreveport, Centenary College

Average household income ranges from $40,000-$52,000, with significant variation by sector.

## Recreation in Shreveport

Shreveport offers genuine recreational value. Cross Lake is a 10,000-acre reservoir within the city limits — popular for fishing, boating, and sunset views. The Red River District features restaurants, music venues, and entertainment. The Shreveport Aquarium and Louisiana State Exhibit Museum offer low-cost family options.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Shreveport affordable compared to other Louisiana cities?**
Yes — Shreveport is more affordable than New Orleans for renters, with lower rent and less tourist-driven pricing inflation. Baton Rouge is roughly comparable. Shreveport's low property taxes make homeownership particularly attractive compared to most US cities.

**What industries are growing in Shreveport?**
Healthcare and logistics are among the most stable growth sectors. The medical corridor anchored by LSU Health continues to expand. Barksdale Air Force Base provides stable military and defense contractor employment. The gaming industry remains large but has faced some headwinds from competition.

**Is Shreveport a good city for military families?**
Yes — Barksdale AFB makes Shreveport a common assignment for Air Force families. The affordable housing market, low property taxes, and access to base amenities make it a financially practical location for military households. Many families purchase homes rather than rent due to the favorable economics.
