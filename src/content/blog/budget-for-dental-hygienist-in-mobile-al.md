Mobile is one of the most affordable mid-size cities for dental hygienists in the Gulf South — but the local PSLF landscape is unusual because the city's two dominant health systems have completely different tax statuses. This guide breaks down salary, cost of living, and the Infirmary Health vs. USA Health distinction that catches many hygienists off guard.

## ⚠️ Critical PSLF Distinction: Infirmary Health vs USA Health

**This is the most important paragraph in this guide.**

Mobile has two dominant health systems, and they have **opposite** PSLF tax statuses:

- **USA Health (University of South Alabama Health)** — Includes USA Health University Hospital, USA Health Children's & Women's Hospital, and USA Health Mitchell Cancer Institute. This is a **public state university system** → fully PSLF-eligible.
- **Infirmary Health** — Includes Mobile Infirmary, Thomas Hospital, North Baldwin Infirmary, and Infirmary Medical Clinics. Infirmary Health IS a 501(c)(3) nonprofit → also PSLF-eligible, but employment must be direct (not via contracted dental groups).
- **Springhill Medical Center** — For-profit (privately owned) → does NOT qualify for PSLF.

A hygienist who switches from USA Health to Springhill Medical mid-career assumes their PSLF clock keeps running — it doesn't. Verify before you move.

## 5 Money Tips for Dental Hygienists in Mobile

1. **File Form PSLF certification annually.** USA Health and Infirmary Health both qualify, but Springhill does not. The two big systems share names with for-profit physician groups that lease space at qualifying hospitals — confirm your W-2 employer is the nonprofit entity itself.

2. **Aggressive savings is possible here.** Mobile's low rent means a 1BR apartment can be 25–28% of take-home rather than the 30%+ that suffocates budgets in Tampa or Atlanta. Push retirement contributions to 10%+ of gross while you can.

3. **Hurricane preparation is a real line item.** Mobile is on the Gulf and has been hit hard (Hurricane Sally 2020 caused billions in damage). Keep $1,500+ in a separate evacuation/repair fund. Renter's insurance is essential and cheap here.

4. **Avoid Daphne if Mobile is your work base.** Cross-bay commutes via I-10 can be 30–45 minutes in peak traffic. The Eastern Shore looks affordable on paper but adds $150+/month in gas and wear.

5. **Watch the corporate DSO consolidation wave.** Heartland Dental, Aspen Dental, and Sage Dental have all expanded into the Mobile market. DSO consolidation can change your benefits package overnight — build a 4-month emergency fund.

## Student Loan Considerations

The average dental hygienist graduates with $20,000–$45,000 in student debt. On an IDR plan at $60,000 income:
- SAVE plan payment: ~$200–$260/month
- Standard 10-year payment: ~$260–$500/month

PSLF eligibility is employer-specific in Mobile:
- **USA Health**: Public university system — PSLF eligible.
- **Infirmary Health**: 501(c)(3) nonprofit — PSLF eligible.
- **Springhill Medical Center**: For-profit — does NOT qualify.
- **Mobile County Health Department / FQHCs (Franklin Primary Health Center)**: PSLF eligible.
- **Private dental practices, DSOs**: For-profit — does NOT qualify.

## FAQ

**Is Mobile a good city for new dental hygienists?**
Yes, if you can tolerate humidity and hurricane risk. The cost-to-income ratio is one of the best in the Southeast — better than Birmingham or Huntsville for first-time renters.

**Why does USA Health qualify for PSLF but Springhill doesn't?**
USA Health is part of the University of South Alabama (public state institution). Springhill Medical is privately owned and for-profit. Hospital names don't tell you tax status — always check.

**How does Mobile compare to Birmingham or Huntsville?**
Mobile pays slightly less but housing is cheaper. Take-home savings rates are comparable. Birmingham has more PSLF-eligible employers (UAB system). Huntsville has higher salaries but higher rent.
