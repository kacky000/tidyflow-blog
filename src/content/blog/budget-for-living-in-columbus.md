# How to Budget for Living in Columbus, Ohio

**Budgeting for living in Columbus** is refreshingly straightforward compared to coastal cities. Ohio's capital sits about 10-15% below the national average for cost of living, and the city has been on a steady upswing — Intel's massive chip factory, growing tech employers like CoverMyMeds and Root Insurance, and a booming food scene have transformed Columbus from a sleepy college town into one of the Midwest's most dynamic metros.

The catch? Ohio has state income tax, property taxes are above average, and four distinct seasons mean your utility bills swing dramatically between summer A/C and winter heating.

Here's your full 2026 Columbus budget guide.

That total is real. A single person can live well in Columbus on $2,500/month — something that's essentially impossible in DC, Boston, or San Francisco. This affordability is Columbus's superpower.

Utilities deserve special attention. Columbus winters bring heating bills of $150-$200/month (natural gas furnaces are standard), while summer A/C pushes electric to $120-$180. Spring and fall are your cheap months at $80-$100 total.

## Budget Allocation by Income

### On $3,000/month (take-home)

- Housing (30%): $900
- Essentials (32%): $960
- Savings (22%): $660
- Fun (16%): $480

At $3,000/month, Columbus is genuinely comfortable. You can afford a decent 1BR in Clintonville, Old North, or Westerville without a roommate. This budget level works for entry-level office workers, teachers, and Ohio State graduate students with stipends.

Use the [50/30/20 rule](/blog/50-30-20-budget-rule-notion) as your framework — Columbus's low costs make it one of the few cities where a 20%+ savings rate is achievable even at modest incomes.

### On $5,000/month (take-home)

- Housing (24%): $1,200
- Essentials (26%): $1,300
- Savings (30%): $1,500

This is where Columbus really shines. A $70,000-$80,000 tech salary here gives you the lifestyle that requires $120,000+ in Austin or Seattle. You can afford a great apartment in Short North or German Village, save aggressively, and still enjoy Columbus's food and bar scene without guilt.

## Columbus Money-Saving Tips

**1. Plan for seasonal utility swings.** Columbus utility bills can vary by $100+/month between seasons. Set your budget to the average ($160/month) and let the cheap spring/fall months build a buffer for winter heating and summer cooling. Track this closely — learn [how to track expenses in Notion](/blog/how-to-track-expenses-in-notion) with seasonal categories.

**2. Use COTA transit and bike paths.** COTA (Central Ohio Transit Authority) offers a monthly pass for about $62. Columbus also has an expanding network of bike paths, and the city is flat — ideal for cycling. Ohio State employees and students ride COTA free with their BuckID.

**3. Shop at Aldi and Kroger.** Aldi is everywhere in Columbus and runs 25-35% cheaper than Giant Eagle. Kroger's loyalty program and fuel points add another layer of savings. The North Market is great for special items but not for weekly shopping.

**4. Take advantage of the OSU ecosystem.** Ohio State brings free or cheap events year-round: lectures, sports (student/staff pricing), Wexner Center for the Arts (free Thursdays), and campus recreation. Even non-students benefit from the cultural infrastructure OSU funds.

**5. Explore Columbus's free parks system.** The Metro Parks system includes 20+ parks with free admission. The Scioto Mile, Highbanks, and Inniswood Gardens offer excellent recreation at zero cost. Franklin Park Conservatory has regular free days too.

## Hidden Costs to Watch

- **Ohio state income tax:** Ranges from 0% (under $26,050) to 3.5% on higher income. Not as painful as California or New York, but it's there — unlike Texas or Nevada.
- **City income tax:** Columbus levies an additional 2.5% municipal income tax. This stacks on top of state tax and catches many newcomers off guard.
- **Winter driving:** Snow tires ($400-$600 for a set), ice scrapers, and occasional salt damage to your car. Budget $300-$500/year for winter-specific vehicle costs.
- **Property tax (if buying):** Franklin County effective property tax rate is about 1.6-2.0%. On a $250,000 home, that's $330-$415/month.

## FAQ

### Is $35,000 a year enough to live in Columbus?

Yes, though it's tight. After federal and Ohio taxes plus the 2.5% city tax, that's about $2,400/month. Doable in neighborhoods like Old North or Franklinton, especially with a roommate. Columbus is one of the few major metros where this salary is genuinely livable.

### How does Columbus compare to other Ohio cities?

Columbus is slightly more expensive than Cincinnati or Cleveland for rent (10-15% higher) but offers stronger job growth, especially in tech. Dayton and Akron are cheaper but have weaker job markets. Columbus hits the sweet spot of affordability plus opportunity.

### Is Columbus good for remote workers?

Excellent. A $100,000 remote salary in Columbus gives you roughly the same lifestyle as $160,000 in San Francisco. Walkable neighborhoods, strong internet infrastructure, and a growing food scene make it a top remote-work destination.
