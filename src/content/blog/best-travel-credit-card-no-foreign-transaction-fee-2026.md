# Best Travel Credit Card With No Foreign Transaction Fee 2026: Top Picks Compared

Finding the **best travel credit card with no foreign transaction fee in 2026** matters more than most people realize. A typical foreign transaction fee runs 3% of every purchase you make abroad — dinners, hotels, museum tickets, rideshares, all of it. On a two-week trip where you spend $3,000 on the card, that silent 3% fee costs you $90. Over a few trips per year, the leak adds up fast.

Every card on this list charges **$0 in foreign transaction fees**. Beyond that common ground, they differ in annual fees, rewards structures, travel perks, and the kind of traveler they suit best. ## Individual Card Reviews

### 1. Chase Sapphire Preferred

**Annual fee:** $95 | **Foreign transaction fee:** $0

The Chase Sapphire Preferred has been a staple of travel-rewards conversations for over a decade, and the 2026 version still holds its ground. You earn 3x points on dining and select streaming, 2x on general travel, and 5x on travel booked through the Chase Travel portal. Points transfer 1:1 to a solid roster of airline and hotel partners — United, Hyatt, Southwest, British Airways, and others — which is where the real value lives. A 60,000-point sign-up bonus after $4,000 in spending within the first three months can be worth $750 or more when transferred to partners strategically.

The $95 annual fee is modest for what you get: trip cancellation/interruption insurance, primary rental car coverage (a genuinely useful perk that many premium cards lack at this price), baggage delay insurance, and no foreign transaction fees. If you travel internationally even once a year and dine out regularly, the card typically pays for itself.

**Watch out for:** The 2x base rate on travel is solid but not exceptional. If you want a higher flat rate and don't care about transfer partners, the Capital One Venture X earns 2x on everything with richer perks — though at a much higher annual fee.

### 2. Capital One Venture X

**Annual fee:** $395 | **Foreign transaction fee:** $0

The Capital One Venture X positions itself as a premium travel card that competes with the Amex Platinum and Chase Sapphire Reserve, and it does so at a lower effective cost than either. You earn 2x miles on every purchase with no category restrictions, 10x on hotels and rental cars booked through Capital One Travel, and a 75,000-mile sign-up bonus after $4,000 in spending within the first three months.

The $395 annual fee sounds steep, but Capital One offsets it with a $300 annual travel credit (applied automatically to Capital One Travel bookings) and 10,000 bonus miles on each account anniversary (worth roughly $100). That brings the effective annual cost down to around $0 for most active travelers. You also get Priority Pass lounge access, Capital One Lounge access, Hertz President's Circle status, and a strong travel insurance package.

Miles transfer to a growing list of partners including Air Canada Aeroplan, Turkish Airlines Miles & Smiles, British Airways, and several others. The transfer ratios and partner list have expanded noticeably since the card launched.

**Watch out for:** If you travel lightly — one or two domestic trips per year — the $395 sticker price is hard to justify even with the credits. The Bank of America Travel Rewards or Wells Fargo Autograph delivers similar no-foreign-transaction-fee benefits at $0 annual cost.

### 3. Bilt Mastercard

**Annual fee:** $0 | **Foreign transaction fee:** $0

The Bilt Mastercard carved out a unique niche: it lets you earn points on rent payments with no transaction fee, something almost no other card offers. You earn 1x on rent (up to 100,000 points per year), 3x on dining, 2x on travel, and 1x on everything else — provided you make at least five transactions per statement cycle to activate point earning.

For travelers, the real draw is the transfer partner list. Bilt points transfer 1:1 to American Airlines AAdvantage, United MileagePlus, Hyatt, Air Canada, Turkish Airlines, and more. That is a transfer roster that rivals cards charging $95 to $550 per year, and Bilt charges nothing. Combined with no foreign transaction fees, it becomes a strong secondary (or even primary) travel card for renters who want to turn their largest monthly expense into travel rewards.

**Watch out for:** The five-transaction minimum per cycle catches some people off guard. If you forget and only make three purchases in a billing period, you earn zero points on everything that month — including rent. The base 1x earn rate on non-bonus categories is also low compared to flat 1.5x or 2x cards.

### 4. Bank of America Travel Rewards

**Annual fee:** $0 | **Foreign transaction fee:** $0

If you want a straightforward, no-annual-fee travel card that just works abroad, the Bank of America Travel Rewards card is one of the simplest options. You earn 1.5 points per dollar on every purchase with no category tracking, no activation required, and no caps. Points redeem as a statement credit against travel purchases at 1 cent each, making the math clean: 1.5% back on everything, applied to flights, hotels, car rentals, and similar expenses.

Bank of America Preferred Rewards members can boost that rate. At the Platinum tier ($20,000+ in combined balances), the earn rate jumps to 2.62 points per dollar. At the Diamond tier ($100,000+), it reaches 3.5 points per dollar — which is exceptional for a card with no annual fee. If you already bank with BofA, this card quietly becomes one of the best-value travel cards available.

The 25,000-point sign-up bonus after $1,000 in spending within 90 days is reasonable and easy to hit with normal spending.

**Watch out for:** Without Preferred Rewards status, the flat 1.5x rate is competitive but not exciting. You also cannot transfer points to airline or hotel partners — redemption is statement-credit only, which limits flexibility compared to Chase or Bilt ecosystems.

### 5. Wells Fargo Autograph

**Annual fee:** $0 | **Foreign transaction fee:** $0

The Wells Fargo Autograph earns 3x points on a broad set of everyday categories — travel, dining, gas stations, transit, phone plans, and streaming services — and 1x on everything else. For a card with no annual fee, the category spread is generous. Most $0-fee cards force you to choose between flat-rate simplicity and category bonuses; the Autograph gives you both.

Points are worth 1 cent each when redeemed for travel through the Wells Fargo rewards portal, gift cards, or statement credits. If you hold the Autograph alongside a Wells Fargo Active Cash (2% flat on everything), you can pool points, which creates a useful two-card system: the Autograph handles bonus categories while the Active Cash covers the rest.

The 20,000-point welcome offer after $1,000 in spending within three months is modest but achievable, and there are no foreign transaction fees — making the Autograph a reasonable travel companion for mid-frequency travelers.

**Watch out for:** No transfer partners. Points are locked into Wells Fargo's redemption ecosystem, which offers decent but not outsized value. Power users who want to transfer points to airlines will find the Chase or Bilt ecosystems more rewarding.

### 6. Discover it Miles

**Annual fee:** $0 | **Foreign transaction fee:** $0

The Discover it Miles earns 1.5x miles on every purchase with no caps and no category tracking. What makes it unusual is the **first-year miles match**: Discover automatically doubles all the miles you earn in your first 12 months. If you spend $20,000 in year one, you earn 30,000 miles normally — and Discover adds another 30,000 as a match, giving you 60,000 miles total. That effectively turns the card into a 3% flat-rate card for the entire first year.

After the first year, the card settles into a standard 1.5x earn rate, which is solid for a no-annual-fee card. Miles redeem at 1 cent each as a statement credit against purchases, or as cash deposited to your bank account.

**Watch out for:** Discover's acceptance network is narrower than Visa or Mastercard, particularly outside the United States. While Discover has expanded its international reach through the Diners Club and JCB networks, you will still encounter merchants — especially in parts of Europe, Southeast Asia, and South America — that do not accept Discover. Carrying a backup Visa or Mastercard when traveling internationally is a practical necessity.

### 7. U.S. Bank Altitude Go

**Annual fee:** $0 | **Foreign transaction fee:** $0

The U.S. Bank Altitude Go leads with a **4x points rate on dining**, which is one of the highest dining rewards rates available on any no-annual-fee card. You also earn 2x on groceries, streaming services, and gas stations, and 1x on everything else. If dining is a significant part of your travel spending — restaurants, street food, cafes abroad — this card pulls ahead of most competitors in that specific category.

Points are worth 1 cent each toward travel or as statement credits. The 20,000-point sign-up bonus after $1,000 in spending within 90 days is straightforward. No foreign transaction fees make it a viable card to carry internationally, especially in food-centric destinations.

**Watch out for:** The card shines on dining but its travel earn rate is only 1x, which is below average. If you spend heavily on flights and hotels, a card with a travel bonus category (like the Autograph's 3x) will serve you better. The Altitude Go works best as a dining-specific card in a multi-card setup.

## Tips to Maximize Your Travel Card Abroad

1. **Always pay in local currency.** When a merchant offers to charge you in USD (dynamic currency conversion), decline. Their exchange rate is almost always worse than your card issuer's rate.

2. **Set travel notifications.** Most issuers let you set travel alerts through the app. This reduces the chance of a fraud hold blocking your card mid-trip.

3. **Carry a backup card on a different network.** If your primary card is Visa, keep a Mastercard in reserve. Some countries and merchants have network preferences.

4. **Use mobile wallets where supported.** Apple Pay and Google Pay are widely accepted in Europe, Australia, and parts of Asia. Tap-to-pay is faster and avoids handing your physical card to a stranger.

5. **Check point transfer rates before redeeming.** If your card offers transfer partners, compare the value of transferring to an airline versus taking a statement credit. Transfer redemptions often yield 1.5 to 2.5 cents per point — significantly more than the standard 1 cent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a foreign transaction fee?

A foreign transaction fee is a surcharge — usually 3% of the purchase amount — that your card issuer adds when you make a purchase in a foreign currency or through a foreign bank. Every card on this list waives that fee entirely.

### Do these cards work internationally?

Yes, though acceptance varies by network. Visa and Mastercard are accepted almost everywhere globally. Discover has limited international coverage, so it is best used as a secondary card abroad. Capital One, Chase, and most other issuers on this list use Visa or Mastercard networks.

### Is a $0 annual fee card enough for international travel?

For many travelers, yes. Cards like the Bilt Mastercard, Wells Fargo Autograph, and Bank of America Travel Rewards offer no foreign transaction fees, reasonable rewards, and enough travel protections for occasional international trips. Premium cards like the Venture X add lounge access and higher earn rates, but the extra perks only justify the fee if you use them.

### Can I use multiple travel cards together?

Absolutely. A common strategy is to pair a flat-rate card (like the BofA Travel Rewards at 1.5x on everything) with a category card (like the Altitude Go at 4x on dining). Use each card where it earns the most, and you maximize your total return without paying multiple annual fees.

### Do sign-up bonuses count toward the first year's value?

They can make a significant difference. A 60,000-point bonus from the Chase Sapphire Preferred is worth at least $600 in travel — far more than the $95 annual fee. Even modest bonuses of 20,000 points ($200 value) on no-fee cards are worth capturing if you would have spent the qualifying amount anyway.
