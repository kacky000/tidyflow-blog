But they take opposite approaches to pricing, and that difference has real consequences depending on how much you invest and how you use the platform. This is not investment advice. The goal here is a straightforward comparison so you can decide which structure makes more sense for your situation.

## Fee Structure

**Schwab** advertises a $0 advisory fee, which sounds like a no-brainer — but the catch is the required cash allocation. Schwab holds 6% to 29.4% of your portfolio in cash, earning interest that Schwab keeps. This "cash drag" effectively functions as an indirect fee. On a $50,000 portfolio with a 10% cash allocation, you're holding $5,000 in cash that earns little while the market moves. Independent analyses have estimated the implicit cost at roughly 0.15%–0.52% depending on allocation, market conditions, and account size.

**Vanguard Digital Advisor** charges approximately 0.20% per year in net advisory fees on top of the underlying fund expense ratios. The funds are Vanguard's own index funds, which average around 0.05%, so the total all-in cost is roughly 0.15%–0.20% annually. That's transparent and predictable. For smaller accounts, this is often a better deal than Schwab's cash drag on proportionally large balances.

## Account Minimums and Requirements

This is where the two platforms diverge sharply for newer investors.

**Schwab Intelligent Portfolios** requires a $5,000 minimum to open an account. There's no way around this. For someone starting out with $500 or $1,000, Schwab simply isn't an option.

**Vanguard Digital Advisor** dropped its minimum to $100, making it one of the more accessible robo-advisors among established brokerages. That said, Vanguard's broader ecosystem still skews toward investors with meaningful assets — the platform's features and communication style reflect that.

Neither platform charges trading commissions or account maintenance fees beyond what's described in the fee structure above.

## Investment Approach and Portfolio Options

**Schwab** builds portfolios from 51 ETFs across asset classes including U.S. and international equities, fixed income, commodities, and real estate. The diversification is broad, but the ETF selection includes some Schwab-affiliated funds, which is worth noting since Schwab collects revenue from them. Portfolios are allocated based on a risk questionnaire and rebalanced automatically when allocations drift.

**Vanguard Digital Advisor** keeps things simpler. It uses four core Vanguard index funds — Total Stock Market, Total International Stock, Total Bond Market, and Total International Bond. If you're a fan of the three-fund or four-fund portfolio philosophy, this approach will feel familiar. The simplicity also makes the portfolio easier to understand and track over time.

## Tax-Loss Harvesting

**Schwab Intelligent Portfolios** offers automatic tax-loss harvesting, but only for accounts with $50,000 or more. Below that threshold, you don't get access to the feature. For taxable accounts above $50,000, this is a meaningful benefit — Schwab scans for harvesting opportunities daily and replaces sold positions with similar ETFs to maintain exposure while locking in losses for tax purposes.

**Vanguard Digital Advisor** does not offer automated tax-loss harvesting as of 2026. Vanguard has historically been cautious about this feature, partly because its own research has questioned how much value it adds after taxes and transaction costs in many scenarios. If tax-loss harvesting is a priority — particularly for a large taxable account — Schwab has a clear edge here.

## Planning Tools and Human Advisor Access

**Schwab Intelligent Portfolios** includes basic goal-setting tools within the platform. For actual human financial planning support, you'd need to upgrade to **Schwab Intelligent Portfolios Premium**, which charges a one-time $300 planning fee plus $30 per month for unlimited access to a CFP. That's a meaningful additional cost for what's supposed to be a "free" robo-advisor.

**Vanguard Digital Advisor** includes access to Vanguard's advisory team within its fee structure, though interactions are more limited than a full-service advisor relationship. Vanguard also has **Personal Advisor Services** as an upgrade path, which pairs you with a dedicated advisor for a 0.30% annual fee on qualifying balances. For investors who want occasional human guidance without a separate monthly subscription, Vanguard's model can feel more integrated.

## Cash Management and Banking

**Schwab** has a fully featured banking arm — Schwab Bank — with checking accounts, debit cards, and no foreign transaction fees. If you want your investment account and banking under one roof, Schwab is one of the better options in that regard. The downside circles back to the cash drag: that required cash allocation sits in a Schwab Bank Savings account earning yields that are lower than what you'd find at a high-yield savings account elsewhere.

**Vanguard** is primarily an investment platform and doesn't have a traditional banking component. You'll need a separate bank account for cash management. Vanguard does offer a money market fund option for cash, which can be more competitive than Schwab's forced savings allocation, but the overall banking integration is minimal.

## Verdict: Which Robo-Advisor Is Better for You?

There's no universal answer — it depends on your balance size, whether you want tax-loss harvesting, and how much you value portfolio simplicity.

**Choose Schwab Intelligent Portfolios if:**
- You have $50,000+ to invest and want automated tax-loss harvesting
- You prefer a $0 stated advisory fee and understand the cash drag tradeoff
- You want banking and investing integrated in one platform
- You already have a Schwab brokerage account

**Choose Vanguard Digital Advisor if:**
- You're starting with less than $5,000 and want a low-cost robo-advisor
- You prefer complete transparency in fees over a "free" but complex pricing structure
- You're comfortable with a simple four-fund index portfolio
- Tax-loss harvesting isn't a priority for your situation

## FAQ

**Does Schwab Intelligent Portfolios really charge no fees?**
There's no advisory fee in the traditional sense, but Schwab requires a cash allocation (6%–29.4% of your portfolio) that earns interest paid to Schwab rather than to you. This is an indirect cost that functions like a fee. The higher your cash allocation, the more you're effectively paying.

**What is the minimum to open Vanguard Digital Advisor?**
As of 2026, the minimum is $100. This makes it accessible to newer investors, though Vanguard's platform is generally designed for those building toward larger balances over time.

**Does Vanguard Digital Advisor offer tax-loss harvesting?**
No. Vanguard Digital Advisor does not include automated tax-loss harvesting. If this is important to you, Schwab (for accounts $50,000+), Wealthfront, or Betterment are alternatives worth evaluating.

**Can I talk to a human advisor with either platform?**
Yes, but the structure differs. Vanguard includes limited advisor access within its fee. Schwab offers human planning through its Premium tier, which costs $300 upfront plus $30/month — an additional cost on top of the base service.

**Which is better for a taxable brokerage account?**
For large taxable accounts where tax-loss harvesting adds value, Schwab's offering at $50,000+ is competitive. For smaller taxable accounts, Vanguard's transparent fee structure and low-cost index funds may result in lower total costs.
